## Blind typed playtest from a checkpoint: session `typed-8-lawanda`
**Who:** Claude (Opus 5), blind playtester, typed parser commands only **Date:** 2026-09-11  **Session:** `typed-8-lawanda` (`node scripts/playtest.mjs --session typed-8-lawanda --new --checkpoint lawanda`)

### Briefing
The briefing says I'm a Stellar Patrol ensign whose ship blew up; on day one I fixed a communications transmitter, reactivated a robot named Floyd, and today (day 2) rode a shuttle to Lawanda Platform carrying a bedistor, pliers, a laser and some access cards. I feel "a bit weak and slightly flushed" (sounds like an illness creeping in), and my plan was to explore the complex beyond this platform. It is clear and well written; the only thing it leaves me guessing about is what the bedistor and pliers are for, and whether my survival kit's food back in Storage West is going to matter.

### Attempt 1, turns 256-266: Getting my bearings at Lawanda Platform
I started with a `look` to re-read the room, then `inventory`. Two small surprises right away. First, `inventory` cost a turn and a surprisingly large chunk of clock (time jumped 3028 to 3046), which makes me a little nervous about idle commands given the illness hint in the briefing. Second, I am carrying a **laser** with a dial set to 5 and an "old battery", and the briefing never mentions where I got it. Useful, presumably, but it means my "memory" has a hole.

The platform description quotes a faded sign ("Shutul Platform -- Lawanda Staashun"), so I typed `examine sign`, and the game said **"You can't see any sign here!"** That is a classic scenery miss: the noun is right there in the room text. `examine escalator` worked but was a bare "nothing special". `examine laser` gave a nice, informative description (six settings, currently 5, depression on top holding the battery); `examine dial` and `examine battery` also worked.

`climb escalator` put me on an intermediate Escalator room (broken stairway), `up` again reached **Fork**, a hallway forking northeast and southeast. Floyd tagged along with his "Floyd here now!" entrance. Next I will try the northeast branch first.

### Attempt 1, turns 267-283: The Infirmary and a dose of bitter medicine
From Fork I went `ne` into **Systems Corridor West** (bends east/southwest, doorway northwest, narrow stairs down to the north). The northwest doorway led to an **Infirmary** with beds, baffling equipment, a small red spool on a bed and a translucent medicine bottle. Given that I woke "weak and slightly flushed", this felt like exactly what I was looking for.

`take bottle and spool` got the spool but refused the bottle: "Your load is too heavy." I liked the follow-up hint: *"(For the medicine bottle: dropping the good ninety-ohm bedistor would make enough room.)"* That is a genuinely helpful inventory message, though I would have preferred to just sip the medicine where it stood. `drink medicine` without holding it said "You're not holding the medicine bottle", so I did `drop bedistor`, `take bottle`, `open bottle`, `drink medicine`: "The medicine tasted extremely bitter. After a few moments the fever breaks, and you feel your strength returning." Good. The label ("Dizeez supreshun medisin -- eksperimentul") says *suppression*, though, so I expect the illness to come back; I'll keep an eye out for more. No points for it (still 42).

Meanwhile Floyd found a rusted robot breast plate engraved "Lazarus", sobbed, and ran out of the room. A lovely, sad little moment. The spool is labelled "Simptumz uv Xe Dizeez", so somewhere there must be a spool reader. I emptied the bottle, dropped it, took the bedistor back. `examine beds` answered in the singular ("the bed"), fine.

### Attempt 1, turns 284-296: Achilles, a robot-sized door, and Floyd earns his keep
`diagnose` now reports "perfect health, well-rested, well-fed", confirming the medicine worked, at least for now. I took the narrow stairway `down` from Systems Corridor West into a dim **Repair Room**: locked cabinets, strange machines, a dead robot at the foot of the stairs, and "a very small doorway" in the north wall. Floyd identified the corpse as Achilles, the repair robot with a bad foot. Great flavour.

Parser niggles here: `search robot` asked "Which do you mean, the Floyd or the broken robot?" ("the Floyd" reads oddly for a named character), and `examine doorway` asked "the small doorway or the doorway?" even though this room only describes one doorway (the other must be the stair opening; the question confused me more than it helped). `search broken robot` found nothing.

`north` gave "It is a robot-sized doorway -- a bit too small for you." I have a robot. `floyd, go north` produced a charming scene: squeals, a rubber ball that fell apart, and "Nothing else interesting inside. Just a shiny fromitz board." The mention of a *shiny* board, in a game where I'm carrying a bedistor and pliers, screamed "part", so I typed `floyd, get the fromitz board` and he fetched it ("you just manage to catch it before it smashes"). No points yet, but it feels important: a square seventeen-centimetre circuit board. Floyd then announced "Floyd going exploring. See you later" and came back two turns later.

### Attempt 1, turns 297-313: Fixing Planetary Defense
Climbing back `up`, I got the status line "You notice that you feel a bit weak and slightly flushed" again, only ~18 turns after the medicine. So the medicine really is just a suppressant, and the disease is a running clock. That raises the stakes on everything.

`east` to **Systems Corridor**, doorway north labelled "Planateree Deefens". Inside, a blinking light: "Surkit Boord Faalyur. WORNEENG: xis boord kuntroolz xe diskriminaashun surkits", and a closed access panel. I have a spare fromitz board, so this was obvious. `open panel` asked "the access panel or the set of controls?" (calling the controls a "panel" is fair, but it caught me twice, since `look in panel` asked the same). `open access panel` showed four boards: first, fourth, third, second (odd listing order).

I examined all four: identical text. No sign of which one had failed. `take first board` gave me a shock; `take first board with pliers` answered only "Nothing interesting happens", which read as "pliers don't matter here". On a hunch I tried `take second board`: it slid out, and only in `inventory` did I learn it was the **fried** one. Then `put shiny board in access panel`: "The card clicks neatly into the socket. The warning lights stop flashing." **+6, score 48.** Good payoff, but I found the faulty board by luck. I dropped the fried board and closed the panel.

### Attempt 1, turns 314-324: Course Control and the bedistor swap
I typed `save` before moving on (Floyd: "Oh boy! Are we gonna try something dangerous now?", which made me laugh). Then `south`, `east` to **Systems Corridor East**: a large doorway east, smaller ones north ("Planateree Kors Kontrool") and south.

**Course Control** had two blinking lights, "Bedistur Faalyur!" and "Kritikul diivurjins frum pland kors", plus a metal cube. Now I understood why I was carrying a good ninety-ohm bedistor and pliers. `open cube` revealed a fused bedistor; `take fused bedistor` gave "It seems to be fused to its socket", a great nudge; `take fused bedistor with pliers`: "With a tug, you manage to remove the fused bedistor." `put good bedistor in cube`: "The warning lights go out and another light goes on." **+6, score 54.** The new light reads "Kors diivurjins minimiizeeng" (course divergence minimising). This was the smoothest puzzle so far: every refusal pointed at the next step, and the briefing's inventory set it up perfectly.

### Attempt 1, turns 325-336: The Library Lobby terminal
South from Systems Corridor East is the **Library Lobby**: dusty carpet, exits north/south, steps up west to a wide doorway, a booth east, "a small object nestled among the dust", and an off computer terminal. `take small object` asked "the green spool or the red spool?", which gave the object away (not a bad thing: I'd have typed `take spool` next anyway). `examine green spool`: "Helikoptur Opuraateeng Manyuuwul" (helicopter operating manual!).

`turn on terminal` brought up a six-item menu. `press 5` failed ("You can't see any 5 here!") but `type 5` worked, drilling into "Xe Prajekt". I read three entries: the disease escaped from a Center for Advanced Cryogenic Research; the two complexes sit on the twin peak plateaus of Kalamontee and Lawanda with reactors and cryogenic chambers in the mountains below; and the Project's four phases: build, mass-freeze the population, automated research while they sleep, then "reeviivul and inokyuulaashun". So there should be a cure being researched somewhere, and a frozen population. The phonetic spelling is fun but slow to decode; I read aloud in my head and it works.

### Attempt 1, turns 337-348: A teleport booth and the microfilm reader
East of the lobby is **Booth 3**, a tiny room with a 10 cm card slot and brown "1" / beige "2" buttons. `push brown button`: "Teleportaashun buux not aktivaatid." `slide shuttle card through slot`: "Inkorekt awtharazaashun kard...akses deeniid." So I need a teleportation card; noted for later. Nice that "slide X through slot" was understood.

West up the steps is the **Library** with a machine that has "a screen and a small circular opening". `turn on machine`: "Pleez insurt spuul." I typed `put red spool in opening` (using the room's own word) and was asked "the microfilm reader or the doorway?". The *doorway* is an opening? That slowed me down again. `put red spool in reader` worked. The red spool: the disease incubates anywhere from a day to several months, but *once symptoms show, death comes in eight to ten days*; primary symptom high fever, secondary a sharp increase in sleep needed each night. So I have days, not hours, but I'll need more sleep each night. The green spool: helicopter pilots need a **Helicopter Access Card** and a **Control Panel Key**, both from **Transportation Storage**. That's a clear lead.

### Attempt 1, turns 349-358: Into the lab complex
I left both spools in the Library Lobby (`drop red spool and green spool` handled the compound noun list fine) since I had read them. South is **Project Corridor East** (doorways north, south, east, corridor west). East is the **Main Lab**, "the heart of the Project's vast laboratory complex", with exits west, southwest, a small doorway south, and heavy doors NE and SE. `examine doors` told me their names: bio-lock door and radiation-lock door. `ne` said "The radiation-lock door is closed", so `open radiation-lock door` (hyphenated noun parsed fine). I saved, then walked through **Radiation Lock West** to **Radiation Lock East**, where a sign warns "Raadeeaashun suuts must bee worn beeyond xis point." I have no suit, and in a game that already gave me a fatal disease I'm not going to test it. Backing out to try the bio-lock and the other exits.

### Attempt 1, turns 359-378: A glimpse of the Bio Lab, and a treasure-trove closet
Back in the Main Lab I opened the bio-lock and went through **Bio Lock West** (the western door closed silently behind me) to **Bio Lock East** (small typo: "The is the second half..."). `look through window`: a dim lab, a blue glow from a crack in the north wall, "Shadowy, ominous shapes move about", and a magnetic-striped card just inside the door, which Floyd identified as a **miniaturization booth access card**. Those shapes worry me; `examine shapes` gave "You can't see any shapes here!" (fair, they are behind glass, but a line saying "you can only see them dimly through the window" would be kinder). I left without opening the door.

The small doorway south of the Main Lab is **Lab Storage**: a pale blue lab uniform on a rack and a fresh laser battery on a shelf. `search lab uniform` turned up a **piece of paper** ("Kombinaashun tuu Konfurins Ruum: 982") and a **teleportation access card**, exactly what Booth 3 wanted. The uniform's logo is a flame over a sleep chamber.

Battery swap was a little clunky: `take battery` asked "new battery or old battery?" (I'd never mean the one already inside my laser), and `take old battery` said "You already have it." even though it was seated in the laser. `drop old battery` did take it out and drop it, then `put new battery in laser` seated the fresh one. Result is right, but it took five commands for a two-step swap.

### Attempt 1, turns 379-391: The broken Computer, the miniaturization booth and a rippling mural
Southwest of the Main Lab is the **Computer Room** with a glowing red light. Floyd: "Uh oh. Computer is broken... Computer is the most important part of the Project." `read output` gave the key page: research and drug production all at 100%, drug testing at 99.985%, "Proojektid tiim tuu reeviivul prooseedzur: 0 daaz", then "ALURT! Malfunkshun in Sekshun 384! Sumuneeng reepaar roobot." The repair robot is Achilles, dead at the foot of the stairs. So the cure is essentially finished and the computer fault is the only thing stopping it. The story clicked into place here, which felt great.

South of the Computer Room is the **Miniaturization Booth** (slot plus numeric keypad). Floyd whispered "Are we going to teleport into the computer like Achilles always used to do?" So: get the miniaturization card from the Bio Lab floor, come here, shrink into the computer and fix Section 384. The Bio Lab's "ominous shapes" are the obstacle.

West is the **ProjCon Office**: a Project Control logo and a garish mural that "seems to ripple now and then, as though a breeze were blowing behind it." Floyd doesn't remember it. Secret passage, surely. But `look behind mural` answered "It won't budge." (a push/move response to a look command), and `move mural` also "won't budge". Parking it. North of the office is **Project Corridor**, a long east-west hall.

### Attempt 1, turns 392-405: Closing the map loop
I mapped the rest of the ground floor. Project Corridor runs west to **Project Corridor West**, which has **SanFac F** (a small dusty washroom) to the west and curves northwest back to the Fork, so the whole complex is a loop: Fork - Systems corridors - Library Lobby - Project Corridor East - Project corridors - Fork. Project Corridor East's south door is just the Computer Room again. It turned dusk at turn 399.

The last unexplored exit was the large doorway east of Systems Corridor East: the **Physical Plant**, "much larger than the one in the Kalamontee Complex" though the complex is smaller. That line smells like a hidden space, but `examine equipment` was the stock "too complicated" reply and `search room` gave "You can't see any room here!" (a common player phrasing that the parser treats as a literal object). Nothing obvious here, so the leads are: teleport booth (I have the card), the Conference Room combination 982 (no Conference Room found on this side yet), the Bio Lab card, the rippling mural, and Transportation Storage for the helicopter.

### Attempt 1, turns 406-419: Riding the teleport network
Back in Booth 3, `slide teleportation card through slot` gave "Redee." I pushed the beige "2" button: "a strange feeling in the pit of your stomach", Floyd squealed and came along, and I was in **Booth 2**. Oddly, the arrival did not print the new room description; I had to `look`. `out` took me to the **Elevator Lobby** in Kalamontee, next to the upper elevator card I'd left there. So the booths connect the two complexes, which is a huge time saver compared with the shuttle.

Booth 2's brown "1" button took me to **Booth 1**, which opens (`out` again) into a **Conference Room** with a round table and a heavy south door. That door "seems to be locked from the other side", so the combination 982 must be keyed from outside and I haven't found that side yet.

On arriving, two status lines: "A growl from your stomach warns that you're getting pretty hungry and thirsty" and, helpfully, "There is still goo in the survival kit you left in Storage West." I like that reminder a lot; it's exactly what my character would remember. Booth 2 drops me in Kalamontee, so the trip is short.

### Attempt 1, turns 420-436: A dinner run to Storage West
Booth 1 -> Booth 2 -> `out` to the Elevator Lobby, then `west` twice (the long hallway walk is compressed into one move with a nice "You walk down the long, featureless hallway for a long time" line). I don't remember the Kalamontee layout exactly, so I poked around: `north` from Dorm Corridor was Dorm D, where I picked up the kitchen access card. I tried `go to storage west` and got "Use compass directions for movement." Fair enough, and a clear message, but for a checkpoint start where my "memory" knows the room names, a go-to would have been welcome. `south`, `west` found Mess Corridor and `north` was **Storage West**.

`eat brown goo` said "You're not holding the survival kit." `take survival kit` said my load was too heavy and suggested dropping the canteen. I chose to drop the pliers instead (their job is done), took the kit and ate the brown goo: "just like delicious Nebulan fungus pudding... moist enough to quench your thirst, too." Hunger and thirst solved in one bite. I'm keeping the kit with the green goo for tomorrow.

### Attempt 1, turns 437-455: Death at the Bio Lab, and a second go with Floyd's plan
I walked back to Booth 2 and pushed the tan "3" button to return to the Library Lobby, then went to **Bio Lock East** and saved. My plan was to open the lab door, snatch the card lying "just inside" and slam it. `open east door`: "Opening the door reveals a Bio-Lab full of horrible mutations... the mutations march into the bio-lock and devour you." **Died** at turn 449, score 54 of 80, rank "Planetary Commodore". Fair enough; the window did say "ominous shapes". The death screen offered RESTART/RESTORE/QUIT.

### Attempt 2 (restore), turns 449-455: Floyd volunteers, and I fumble the knock
`restore` (Floyd: "That part of the game was more fun than this part"). Then one `look`, and this time Floyd peered through the window and **proposed his own plan**: "Floyd will get card. Robots are tough... You open the door, then Floyd will rush in. Then you close door. When Floyd knocks, open door again." So the first time I simply acted one turn too early, before he had spoken. On a first visit the game gives no hint that waiting a turn changes anything; I would never have learned this without dying.

`open door`: Floyd plunged in, was set on by monsters, and yelled for me to close the door. `close door`: growls, a skirmish, a metallic scream. Then I made a mistake of my own: I typed `wait` to give him time. The next turn brought "three fast knocks, followed by the distinctive sound of tearing metal", and I *still* waited (I had lost track of "when Floyd knocks, open door"). The knocks came again, a wild scream, then silence and "an occasional metallic crunch". Through the window the card is still on the floor. I think I've got Floyd killed for nothing. Restoring to try again, opening the door on the first knock.

### Attempt 3 (restore), turns 449-457: Floyd's sacrifice
Restored again. `look through window` triggered Floyd's speech straight away this time. `open door` (Floyd plunges in), `close door` (skirmish, metallic scream), one `wait` for the three knocks, then immediately `open door`: "Floyd stumbles out of the Bio Lab, clutching the mini-booth card. The mutations rush toward the open doorway!" `close door`: "And not a moment too soon!"

Then the scene I half expected and still wasn't ready for: Floyd collapses, torn apart and leaking oil, "Floyd did it ... got card. Floyd a good friend, huh?", and I sing him the Ballad of the Starcrossed Miner as he dies. It is genuinely affecting, easily the high point of the session. **+2** for the scene, and `take mini card` another **+1** (score 57). The card reads "minitcurizaashun akses kard." I saved immediately.

Honest reflection: the knock timing is tight (open on the turn *after* the first knock message) and on my first real try I muffed it by waiting too long. With a save it's fine; without one, a player could lose both Floyd and the card, which may be unrecoverable.

### Attempt 3, turns 468-482: The speck, the relay, and a microbe (death 2)

Restored to just before the red button. The elevator then sat for a few turns. I `wait`ed twice and `look`ed, not sure whether I was meant to act. `open door` got the odd reply "You must be very clever to do that to the cryo-elevator door." followed by the door opening on its own anyway. `north` into the **Cryo-Anteroom** triggered the ending: Veldina, leader of Resida, wakes, thanks me; the S.P.S. Flathead arrives with Blather (demoted to my personal toilet attendant); I'm promoted to Lieutenant First Class and given the antidote; and Floyd, repaired by robot technicians, bounds in with a helicopter key, a reactor elevator card and a paddleball set, "Maybe we can use them in the sequel..." **GAME WON, 80/80, "Galactic Overlord"**, turn 510, day 2.

### Report
I finished the game. Starting from Lawanda Platform at 42 points, I explored the whole Lawanda complex, took the disease-suppression medicine in the Infirmary, had Floyd fetch a spare fromitz board through a robot-sized door, fixed Planetary Defense (+6) and Course Control with the bedistor and pliers (+6), picked up the teleport card and a fresh laser battery from a lab uniform, learned the story from the library terminal, the microfilm reader and the computer printout, let Floyd die retrieving the miniaturization card (+3), shrank into the Computer, lasered the speck out of the relay (+2 odd, +4, +8), got rid of the microbe by throwing the warm laser into the void, came out in the Auxiliary Booth (+4), gassed the Bio Lab, outran the mutants to the Cryo-Elevator (+5) and saw the ending: **80 of 80, turn 510, day 2, about 301 commands, 3 deaths (all restored)**.

The briefing was enough to pick up the thread. Carrying a good bedistor and pliers set up the Course Control puzzle perfectly, and the "weak and flushed" line made the Infirmary an obvious first goal. The one gap was the laser: it's in my inventory and becomes the key item for the endgame, but my "memory" never mentions it. The hunger reminder ("There is still goo in the survival kit you left in Storage West") was an excellent touch for a checkpoint start.

My biggest confusions were all around timing and state I couldn't see. (1) The Bio Lab door: opening it before Floyd has spoken kills you instantly, and Floyd only makes his offer on a later turn; I learned the solution by dying. (2) The knock: I waited one turn too long and lost both Floyd and the card; the knock message reads like "too late" rather than "now". (3) The fungicide is a short window, not a kill; "choking noises" made me wait for them to die, and they ate me. (4) The fried fromitz board looks identical to the good ones until you pull it out. (5) `shoot speck with laser` produced "Sorry, the laser doesn't have a rubber barrel." with +2 points, a baffling non sequitur, while `fire laser at speck` worked. Smaller parser rough edges: scenery named in text but not examinable (the platform sign), "opening"/"panel"/"doorway" disambiguations pointing at the wrong thing, `press 5` refused where `type 5` worked, `take old battery` saying "You already have it" while it's in the laser, and eating/drinking from containers I wasn't holding.

What worked well: Floyd, above all. His hints are delivered in character ("Are we going to teleport into the computer like Achilles always used to do?"), his reactions are funny, and his death and return are the emotional spine of the session. The puzzle chain in the second half is tight and well clued: printout gives the sector number, the laser's warming messages set up the microbe, the memo and gas mask set up the escape, and the PA announcement tells you where to run. Refusals like "fused to its socket" and "a robot-sized doorway" point straight at the answer. The load-limit hint naming exactly what to drop is great.

What I would change: make the deadly timed moments (Bio Lab door, knocks, fungicide window, microbe) signal their timing more clearly; show the fried board as fried; fix the "rubber barrel" misparse; print room descriptions on teleport arrival; accept `press N` on keyboards; and mention the laser in the briefing.

### Verdict
A superb, complete run: the briefing handed me a coherent thread, the Lawanda half is full of satisfying, well-clued puzzles, and Floyd's sacrifice is as moving as anything I've played. The rough spots are a handful of unforgiving timing windows that I could only learn by dying, plus a few parser misreads (the "rubber barrel" reply chief among them); with a save handy they cost minutes, not the game.
