### Report
I played from the very beginning and did not reach Lawanda Platform. I stopped in the Comm Room at the top of the Kalamontee tower on turn 382, with a score of 33 out of 80, after about 438 commands. That count includes a death and restart at turn 13 (I missed the escape pod).

What I got done: escaped the Feinstein; swam from the sinking pod to the island; woke Floyd; used the magnet to fish the key from the crevice; opened the padlock and took the ladder; bridged the rift; collected the kitchen, upper elevator and shuttle access cards; filled my canteen in the Kitchen; slept; rode the upper elevator to the tower; and did the two-step coolant fix in the Comm Room (green, then brown), which sent the distress message (+6).

Biggest blockers:
- I never found the way to the shuttle. The lower elevator and the reactor elevator both need a card I don't have, and the only unexplored places are two pitch-dark rooms (Transportation Supply and the Reactor Access Stairs) and a 0-1000 combination door, with no clue for any of them.
- The flask 'take' failed silently because of weight: the only message was that it 'lands on its side'. I walked seven rooms before I noticed it wasn't with me.
- The weight limit, the 3-hour main corridor and the tower-to-Machine-Shop round trips made a lot of the session ferrying things back and forth.
- Per-room option numbers caught me twice when I chained moves (I also overshot twice with typed directions, which was my own fault).

Best moments:
- The escape sequence, and the pod sinking under me.
- Floyd waking up, his running chatter, and the 'lazy bones' wake-up after the Blather dream.
- The survival UI is excellent: warnings say where my food is and which dorms have bunks, the diagnose button shows the countdowns, and there are one-click 'eat' buttons.
- The Feinstein's last transmission cut off by the explosion.
- Contextual options like 'take with magnet', 'put under spout' and 'put across rift' kept puzzles solvable without guessing verbs.

### Verdict
Yes, mostly. The point-and-click interface let me play the whole opening act without typing a verb: the context-sensitive options, the destination-labelled exits and the survival hints are genuinely good, and I never felt the parser was hiding something. It did let me down twice. Weight and juggling failures are too quiet: the flask, the bedistor and 'take all' grabbing the magnet. And I ended blocked on exploration (finding a card or a light), not on the interface itself.
