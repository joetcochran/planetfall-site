How the rounds worked, and what each one changed, is told on [Playing it](playing-it.md); this round is in [Rounds ten and eleven](playing-it.md#rounds-ten-and-eleven), and every tester's numbers are in [How far each tester got](playing-it.md#how-far-each-tester-got).

- **Start:** from the start (Deck Nine, turn 0), in 3 legs
- **Result:** Did not win. Where play ended: Infirmary, turn 1000, with 53 points.
- **Commands:** 1,212, with one death

**About the numbers.** The diary's report (line 435) says two deaths by starvation in leg 1; the transcript has one death (line 8174). The other rollback in leg 1 was a restore while alive, to undo a smeared access card (diary line 92).

## Blind click-only playtest from the start: session `click-10`
**Who:** Claude (Opus 5), blind mouse-only tester, no prior knowledge used  **Date:** 2026-09-11  **Session:** `click-10` (`node scripts/playtest.mjs --session click-10 --new --click`)

### Leg 1 (commands 1-~350)

#### Turns 1-8: Deck Nine, a brush with no floor to scrub, and an officer who bullies me back to my post

The opening text hands me a job: I am scrubbing a deck with a "Patrol-issue self-contained multi-purpose scrub brush". So the first thing I went looking for was a way to scrub. There isn't one. The brush's `…` menu offers exactly **examine, drop**. There is no `floor` or `deck` scenery tag in the room at all, so there is nothing to point the brush at either. The game's very first sentence describes an action the interface cannot perform. That is a strange note to open on for a mouse-only player, who has no free-text escape hatch — I can't even try `scrub deck` and be told "that's not important".

So I examined what I had. The chronometer doubles as the clock (time 4595, and the status line agrees, so the chronometer is redundant but charming). The ID card has a number and a magnetic stripe — the stripe felt like a promise, so I filed it away. Examining the brush cost me a turn and summoned **Ensign First Class Blather**, who docked me thirty demerits for my polishing.

Blather appears in the option list under a **Characters** group as "Ensign First Class" — *not* "Blather", even though every line of prose calls him Blather. His `104 …` menu is genuinely interesting: **examine, talk to, salute, attack, listen to**. A salute option sitting right there next to an officer handing out demerits reads like an invitation, and I now wish I had taken it before he wandered off. There is also a separate top-level `105 throw at…` entry, which is the first two-object entry I've seen.

I walked east to the Reactor Lobby to scout. Blather followed instantly and threatened forty demerits if I wasn't back in five seconds; trying to go south from there just got me "Ensign Blather pushes you roughly back toward your post." So the opening is a soft cage, and the interface never tells you that — the exits stay listed and clickable, they just fail. Two turns burned learning that.

The Reactor Lobby prose also mixes its vocabularies badly: "To starboard is the Ion Reactor... aft of here is the Auxiliary Control Room. The corridor continues to port", while the buttons say `go south / go east / go west`. Nothing on screen maps starboard→? or aft→?. A keyboard player can type `aft` and find out; I have to click a compass point and hope.

#### Turns 9-14: waiting out the alien, and the ship coming apart

Back at my post I hit `wait` repeatedly, on the theory that a game which pins you to one square wants time to pass. That was right. The Blow'k-bibben-Gordo ambassador ambled in, slimed my deck, offered me Bocci, asked after Admiral Smithers, handed me a brochure (which turns out to be an ad for the game itself), and left — and then a massive explosion opened the escape pod door.

The room's option list changed in a way I want to flag as **good**: `3 open escape pod bulkhead` disappeared the moment the door opened, and a `slime` scenery tag appeared where the ambassador had dripped. The screen tracks the fiction.

Less good: I tried `5 go up` and got "The narrow bulkhead is closed." Only *after* that failure did `20 examine wide bulkhead` and `21 examine narrow bulkhead` appear in the Exits row. And both of those are **examine only** — there is no "open narrow bulkhead" button, ever. So the exits list kept advertising `go east, Reactor Lobby` and `go up` after the explosion had sealed them both, and offered me no way to act on the things blocking them. A mouse player learns the ship is sealed only by clicking doors that don't work. (The failures were free — the turn counter didn't advance — which softens it a lot.)

#### Turns 15-33: into the pod, down to the planet, and out through the water

`14 get in` on the escape pod scored my first points (+3). Inside, `12 get in` the safety web, then `wait` about eleven times through a genuinely lovely descent sequence — the Feinstein exploding, the autopilot hunting, the viewport polarizing against the sun, the island with its plateau of buildings swinging into view.

Then the pod landed, "precariously balanced", and a panel opened revealing a **survival kit** and a **towel**. Here the interface did something I want to praise *and* complain about in the same breath. While I was strapped into the web, the towel offered only `examine / read` and the kit only `examine / open / look inside / search` — **no take on either**. I stared at that for a while assuming the take button had simply been forgotten. It hadn't: the moment I clicked `18 get out of the safety web`, `take` appeared on both objects and a `26 take all (new)` button appeared in the button row. So the UI is modelling "you can't reach those while strapped in" — which is good, careful work — but it communicates that by *silently omitting the button*, which from the player's side is indistinguishable from a bug. One line ("you can't reach that from the web") would turn a suspected bug into a puzzle.

Standing up tipped the pod into the sea. `26 take all` grabbed both items in one turn, which I was grateful for under time pressure. `19 go up / out` failed — "The pod door is closed" — so `2 open escape pod bulkhead`, which flooded the pod, and then out into **Underwater**, and `4 go up` to the **Crag** (+3, score 6).

Small thing worth keeping: in Underwater every exit was labelled `go north, Underwater`, `go south, Underwater`... and only `4 go up` had no destination label. Destination labels on exits are a real navigation aid, and the *absence* of one told me "up is the new place". I went up on that basis and it was right.

#### Turns 34-67: up the cliff, into the ruined complex, and a tour of locked things

The Crag leads up to a **Balcony** with a plaque in phonetic pidgin Galalingua ("Xis stuneeng vuu uf xee Kalamontee Valee..."), then a **Winding Stair**, then a **Courtyard** of a ruined castle. From there the complex opens out: Plain Hall, Rec Area, Rec Corridor, dormitories A-D with their SanFacs, Mess Corridor, Mess Hall, Dorm Corridor, a very long walkway corridor east to a Corridor Junction, and an Elevator Lobby.

What I found, in the order I found it, is essentially a museum of locks:

- **Rec Area**: a door with a combination dial, `14 set to a number <value>`, range 0-1000. No combination anywhere yet.
- **Mess Corridor**: a small door hooked with a steel padlock. The padlock offers **examine only**. `2 open door` tells me "The door cannot be opened until the padlock is removed" — but there is no `remove padlock`, no `unlock padlock`, nothing. At that point I had no key, so fair enough, but the screen gave me no verb at all to aim at the obstacle it had just named.
- **Mess Hall**: a south door with a card slot beside it.
- **Elevator Lobby**: blue door (small elevator), red door (large elevator), both with card slots inside; and a teleport **Booth 2** whose buttons answer "Teleportaashun buux not aktivaatid."

The card slot is where the interface did something genuinely clever and genuinely confusing at the same time. I examined the slot, thought "magnetic stripe, I have a card", opened the ID card's `103 …` menu — **examine, drop, read**. No way to use it. I was about to write this up as a hard dead end. Then I reprinted the full option list and found, sitting *outside* the card's `…` menu as its own top-level entry, `119 slide through slot`. It was there because I was standing next to a slot.

That is exactly the right behaviour, and it is hidden in exactly the wrong place. The `…` menu is advertised in the help text as "a carried thing's everyday actions"; a player who opens it reasonably concludes they have seen that object's verbs. The *interesting* verb — the one the puzzle turns on — lives somewhere else. I would put the contextual action inside the `…` menu too, or at minimum mark the `…` with something ("103 … +1") when the object has an extra contextual action available.

(The card was rejected anyway: "Inkorekt awtharazaashun kard...akses deeniid." Both elevator slots said the same. So there is a better card somewhere.)

Two more things from this stretch. `push blue button` produced "a faint whirring noise from behind the blue door" and nothing else; the door opened two `wait`s later. Nothing on screen said "wait for it" — I only found out because I happened to wait. And in the Upper Elevator the two physical buttons are collapsed into one option row, `button: 13 examine · 14 push up · 15 push down`, which reads oddly (which button am I examining?) but is at least compact.

#### Turns 87-137: hunger, a key I couldn't reach, and the magnet that ruined my ID

A stomach growl at turn 88, with a helpful second line: "The goo in your survival kit would take care of both." `114 eat` on the red goo fixed hunger and thirst in one go. Good: the game names the fix in the same breath as the problem.

Examining the crevice in **Admin Corridor South** revealed a steel key; `13 take` gave "Either the crevice is too narrow, or your fingers are too large." No tool, so I went hunting, and the whole south wing turned out to be the toolbox of the game: **Storage East** (oil can; a cardboard box with a *cracked* fromitz board, a B-series megafuse, a K-series megafuse, a good ninety-ohm bedistor), **Physical Plant**, **Reactor Control** (a lift needing a card, and a pitch-black stairway down — "You might be eaten by a grue"), **Tool Room** (glass flask, U-shaped metal bar, wide-nosed pliers, a portable laser with a six-setting dial and an old battery in it), **Machine Shop** (a chemical dispenser with nine buttons), and **Robot Shop** (a four-foot multiple purpose robot; `12 turn on` scored +2 and produced "a faint hum from somewhere inside").

The inventory limit dominated this whole stretch and it is where the click UI creaked worst. Taking the oil can made me drop the ID card ("Oh, no. The ID card slips from your arms"). Then:

- I clicked the uniform's `106 put in…`, stowed the ID card and the brochure — and on the third attempt got **`That is not one of the options on screen (I don't know the word "106".)`**. The uniform had filled up and its `put in…` entry had silently vanished from the list. The error blames me for an unknown *word*, when what actually happened is that a button I was looking at a moment ago had been removed.
- Exactly the same thing happened again with the cardboard box's `122 put in…` after six items: `I don't know the word "122"`.

Twice in three minutes, a number I had just been shown stopped meaning anything, and the game told me I'd typed gibberish. For a mouse-only player this is the worst class of message there is: it describes a typing mistake I am structurally incapable of making. "The cardboard box is full" would have cost nothing and taught me the rule.

Against that, the *load* messages are excellent: "Your load is too heavy. **Dropping the oil can would make enough room.**" naming a specific item is far better than a bare refusal.

Then the magnet. `16 take curved metal bar` succeeded and immediately warned: "The curved metal bar swings toward the ID card and tugs at it: the bar is a strong magnet. Carried together any longer, it will smear the card's magnetic stripe." My next click was `18 take pliers` — and the card's stripe was smeared. One turn of grace. I don't object to the trap; I object to how the warning is delivered. It arrives as a `>>` event line appended below my action's result, in the same undifferentiated grey as the ambassador's small talk, and then the room redraws with no change to any option anywhere. Nothing in the *clickable* layer changed to say "you are carrying two things that are destroying each other". A status flag on the card row ("ID card (at risk)") or a bright `drop the magnet` button would have caught me. As it is, I am now carrying a card I may have needed, and I won't know until much later.

With the magnet, Admin Corridor South grew a new option: `14 take with magnet` on the key, and it worked. That is the pattern I like best in this UI — the world state adds a verb to the exact object that needs it — and it is the same mechanism as `119 slide through slot`. It just needs to be findable.

#### Turns 141-190: Floyd, the rift, the offices — and the magnet ruins the card that matters

Unlocking the padlock (`15 unlock with key`, which *appeared on the padlock the moment I was carrying a key* — the good pattern again) brought **Floyd** bounding in: the robot I'd switched on in the Robot Shop had come looking for me. He follows me from room to room and chatters.

Floyd's menu is where I expect the next big click-mode problem to land. He offers `130 …` (examine, talk to, salute, attack, listen to, search), `131 look inside`, `132 turn off`, `133 throw at…`, `134 ask about…`. What he does **not** offer is any way to *tell him to do something*. There is no "Floyd, follow me", no "Floyd, get the X", no "give X to Floyd", no "Floyd, wait here". He is plainly built as a companion who does things for you — he's a "multiple purpose robot", he's small enough to go where I can't — and the entire command channel to him is missing from the UI. `134 ask about…` opens a long list of every object in scope, which is conversation, not instruction. I have not yet hit a puzzle that needs him, but if one exists I currently have no button for it.

Past the padlocked door: **Storage West**, with a tin can of "Spam and Egz" and a heavy-duty extendable ladder (+4). The ladder is the heaviest thing in the game so far — `16 take ladder` answered "You'd have to drop the survival kit, the pair of wide-nosed pliers and the oil can first", which is again the good, specific refusal.

Carrying it to the rift in **Admin Corridor** produced a nice little three-step: `open ladder` while holding it fails ("You couldn't possibly extend the ladder while you're holding it"), so `drop`, then `15 open` extends it to eight meters, and **only then** does `18 put across rift` appear on the ladder's option row. Each step's failure told me what to do next, and the option list grew the verb at the exact moment it became possible. That is the UI at its best. Crossing scored +4 and also popped a `16 sleep` button into the button row the instant the game decided I was weary — a good example of a state change surfacing as a clickable.

Beyond the rift: **Admin Corridor North** with three portals — a pitch-dark **Transportation Supply**, a **Plan Room** with maps of the "Kalamontee Kompleks" (here) and a second "Lawanda Kompleks", one installation of it buried deep underground — and west, a **Small Office** (desk: kitchen access card, upper elevator access card) and a **Large Office** (desk: shuttle access card).

And then the magnet fired again, on the card that actually matters.

I took the shuttle access card. The same `>>` line appeared: "The curved metal bar swings toward the shuttle access card and tugs at it... Carried together any longer, it will smear the card's magnetic stripe." I had already seen this exact trap once, forty turns earlier, and I *still* walked into it, because my next click was a movement — `1 go east` — and by the time the room redrew, the card was smeared. Examining it: "The magnetic stripe on the back looks smeared, as if the curved metal bar had been stuck to it while you carried them together."

I want to be precise about why this beat me twice. The warning is correct, clear and in advance. But it is delivered in the *narrative* channel, appended under the result of whatever I just clicked, in the same visual register as "Floyd recalls the time he bruised his knee." Meanwhile the *clickable* channel — the thing a mouse player actually reads, because it's the thing they act through — showed no change at all: the magnet and the card sat in the carried list as two ordinary rows, no marker, no warning, and no `drop the magnet` shortcut anywhere near them. In a typed game you re-read the last paragraph before typing. In a click game you scan the option list and click. The option list said everything was fine.

#### Attempt 2 (restore), turns 67-115: the rollback, and why I paid 50 commands for it

The shuttle access card is almost certainly the gate to the whole second complex on that Plan Room map. A smeared one is a dead run. And because **the game has a single save slot**, that decision could not be deferred: the moment anyone in this session pressed `save` again, the clean pre-smear state would be gone forever. So it had to be made immediately, by me, before I did anything else.

I restored. The save was 115 turns back, at the Elevator Lobby (turn 67, score 6) — I had been saving far too rarely, precisely because the single slot makes every save a destructive act and I kept putting it off. That is worth saying plainly: **the one-slot rule made me save less often, which made the eventual rollback enormous.**

The redo cost 50 of my 350 commands. It was nearly painless, and for a reason that deserves credit: **option numbers are stable per room across a restore.** The help text promises this and it holds. I retraced the whole route — Robot Shop, Tool Room, crevice, padlock, Storage West, ladder, rift, offices — almost entirely from numbers I had written down, with barely any re-reading of option lists. A click UI that re-shuffled its numbers would have made that redo three times longer.

This time I dropped the magnet the instant the key was out of the crevice, and I left my Patrol ID card behind in the Tool Room so it couldn't be smeared either. All three access cards are clean. Score **19** (the two office cards I'd never bothered to pick up the first time are worth +1 each), and I saved immediately in the Large Office.

One more thing the redo taught me: the robot wakes up as Floyd only *after* you leave the Robot Shop — "the robot you were fiddling with bounds into the room" arrived three turns later, in the Tool Room. The first time round I clicked `12 turn on`, got "Nothing happens", and walked off assuming it had failed.

#### Turns 116-160: the tower, the Comm Room, and the kitchen

With clean cards the game opened up fast. `142 slide through slot` in the Upper Elevator got "Elevator enabled", `14 push up` closed the door and started moving, and then a new button appeared in the button row: **`16 wait for the elevator`**. Clicking it waited repeatedly, all by itself, until the car actually arrived and the door opened. That is the single best interface idea I've met in this session — it collapses "click wait, read, click wait, read, click wait" into one intention-shaped button. Please do more of this.

(One sour note attached to it: after riding up and walking around the tower, I came back, clicked `15 push down` and got "Nothing happens. The slot beside the buttons stays dark." The card authorisation had lapsed and nothing said so; I had to work out that I needed to slide the card again. Also, my remembered `16` was gone — `wait for the elevator` only exists while the car is moving — and reusing it gave the "I don't know the word 16" message again.)

The top of the shaft is the **Tower Core**, with a **Helipad** above (a rusted-solid helicopter with a locked control panel — that oil can in the Tool Room is clearly waiting for it), an **Observation Deck** (another island about 20 km east: the Lawanda complex from the Plan Room map), and northeast the **Comm Room**, which is the densest room in the game so far:

- a receive console whose `11 push` plays the Feinstein's last transmission before it blew up;
- a send console whose screen reads "Planitwiid plaag haz struk entiir popyuulaashun. Tiim iz kritikul. Eemurjensee asistins reekwestid" — so this is a distress call that was never answered;
- a flashing "Malfunkshun in Sendeeng Kuulint Sistum";
- a funnel-shaped hole labelled "Kuulint Sistum Manyuuwul Oovuriid";
- and an **enunciator** panel of seven coloured lights — red, blue, green, yellow, gray, brown, black — with the **green** one flashing.

Those are exactly the colours of the Machine Shop's chemical dispenser: "KUULINTS 1-4" in red/blue/green/yellow and "KATALISTS 1-3" in gray/brown/black. So: catch green coolant in the glass flask from the Tool Room, carry it up, pour it down the funnel. I did not have the commands left to run that errand, but I am confident in it, and I want to record that the click UI made that deduction *possible* — the enunciator and the dispenser both name their colours, and both colour sets are spelled out in the option list.

Back downstairs the kitchen access card opened the **Kitchen** (+4): a "Hii Prooteen Likwid Dispensur" with an **octagonal niche** beneath a spout. Pushing the button spills brown liquid on the floor. The **canteen** from the Mess Hall is octagonal. That one is not subtle and the UI states both shapes plainly.

#### Turns 161-174: death by not noticing I was starving, and a second restore

I was weary, so I walked to Dorm D, saved, got in the bed, clicked `13 sleep`, and waited. The dream sequence (Blather on a cliff, the brush sailing into the void) is lovely. I woke in the morning, clicked `14 get out of the bed`, clicked `6 diagnose` to check my state — and the diagnose came back "You feel well-rested. You seem to be **noticeably** thirsty and hungry", followed immediately by "**You collapse from extreme thirst and hunger. You have died.**"

In the same breath. "Noticeably" and dead, one line apart. I was carrying a survival kit with two blobs of goo in it the entire time.

Some of this is on me — I had been trimming the tool's output to the first few lines while batching moves, so I very likely clipped a hunger warning. But the part that isn't on me is what the *option list* was doing during all of it. Hunger is a countdown to death, and the clickable layer never once reflected it. There is a `9 diagnose` button and a `13 sleep` button that appears when you're weary — but no `eat` button ever appears when you're hungry, even though the fix is in your own inventory and the game knows it (it told me so, in prose, at the first growl: "The goo in your survival kit would take care of both"). To eat, I have to remember which blob is which number inside a container inside my inventory. Weariness gets promoted to a one-click button; starvation, which actually kills you, does not.

I restored (to the save I'd made at Dorm D, turn 164), ate the brown goo *first* this time, then slept. Waking up added one more wrinkle worth flagging: "While you slept, the things you were carrying slipped to the floor beside you." Everything I own — all three access cards included — was on the floor. `31 take all` picked up six things and then, on the seventh, produced "Oh, no. The upper elevator access card slips from your arms while taking the shuttle access card and both tumble to the ground." A one-click convenience that silently drops things when you hit the weight limit is a trap in a game where one of those things is a run-ending item. I dropped the scrub brush (which the game has been begging me to abandon since turn 104) and re-took both cards by hand.

Ending the leg here: Dorm D, morning of Septem 7, score **27**, saved, everything accounted for.

### Leg 1 handover

**Where I am:** Dorm D, morning, turn 174, score 27, one death (starvation, recovered by restore). **The save slot currently holds this exact spot** — Dorm D, turn 174-ish, score 27, all three access cards clean and carried. Please save early and often; see the warning below about what a stale save costs.

**What I carry:** chronometer (worn), Patrol uniform (worn), survival kit containing **one** blob of green goo (I've eaten the red and the brown), a tin can of "Spam and Egz" (unopened), the steel key, and the **kitchen / upper elevator / shuttle access cards, all three unsmeared**.

**What I was about to do:** the Comm Room coolant repair. Fetch the **glass flask** from the Tool Room, take it to the **Machine Shop** dispenser, and make the coolant the Comm Room enunciator is asking for — the **green** light was flashing, and the dispenser's coolant buttons are red/blue/green/yellow ("KUULINTS 1-4") with catalysts gray/brown/black ("KATALISTS 1-3"). Then carry it up the Upper Elevator to the **Comm Room** and pour it into the funnel-shaped hole labelled "Kuulint Sistum Manyuuwul Oovuriid". Watch the enunciator — I suspect the colour it asks for changes.

**Things I worked out that you should not have to rediscover:**

- **The magnet will destroy your access cards.** The curved metal bar in the Tool Room is a strong magnet; carrying it alongside any magnetic-stripe card smears the stripe *one turn* after the warning. Use it only to fish the key out of the crevice in Admin Corridor South, and **drop it in that room the moment you have the key**. I lost a whole run to this.
- **Only one save slot.** Every save destroys the previous one, so a mistake you notice late may be unrecoverable. Decide immediately when something goes wrong.
- **Room option numbers are stable**, including across a restore. Write them down; a rollback is then mostly a walk.
- **Eat before you're told twice.** Hunger kills with almost no clickable warning. Keep goo on you. The kitchen dispenser (below) is probably the sustainable food supply.
- **Sleeping dumps your entire inventory on the floor.** Pick it all up, by hand if `take all` starts dropping things.
- **The elevator needs the card re-slid every trip**, and `wait for the elevator` only exists while the car is in motion.

**Open leads, roughly in order of promise:**

1. **Coolant → Comm Room funnel** (above). Also: the Comm Room is how you'd answer a distress call — the Feinstein was hailing "frequency 48.5" and this planet's people died of a plague asking for help.

The canteen is currently lying on the floor of the **Tool Room** where I dropped it. This is almost certainly the permanent answer to hunger and thirst.
3. **The laser** (Tool Room, dial with six settings, contains an old battery) for the two pitch-black rooms: **Reactor Access Stairs** (down from Reactor Control) and **Transportation Supply** (north from Admin Corridor North). Test whether the old battery still works before relying on it.
4. **The oil can** (Tool Room) for the helicopter on the Helipad — "everything is covered with a thick layer of rust" and its control panel is "closed and locked".
5. **The shuttle access card** and the second island 20 km east (Lawanda Kompleks; the Plan Room map shows one of its two installations buried deep underground). I have not found the shuttle itself yet — likely behind the **red door / Lower Elevator** in the Elevator Lobby, or via the **teleport booths** ("Teleportaashun buux not aktivaatid" — activating them is probably one of the four broken systems).
6. **The combination door** in the **Rec Area**: dial, 0-1000, `14 set to a number <value>`. No combination found anywhere yet.
7. **Floyd.** He follows you and wanders off on his own. I never found a way to *instruct* him — see friction #18. If you find a puzzle that needs a small robot to go somewhere you can't, check whether the UI has grown a way to ask him, because it hadn't by turn 174.
8. **Systems Monitors** (west of Admin Corridor) is the scoreboard: LIIBREREE, REEAKTURZ and LIIF SUPORT are green; **PLANATEREE DEFENS, PLANATEREE KORS KUNTROOL, KUMUUNIKAASHUNZ and PRAJEKT KUNTROOL** are malfunctioning. That is almost certainly the to-do list for the whole game. Check it after each repair.

### Leg 2 (commands ~341-~700)

#### Turns 175-184: a fever nobody warned me about

I picked up exactly where leg 1 left off — Dorm D, morning, score 27, three clean access cards — and set off for the Tool Room to fetch the glass flask (for the coolant) and the canteen (for the Kitchen dispenser). I promptly went the wrong way: Dorm Corridor → Mess Corridor → Rec Corridor → Plain Hall, which is the *castle* half of the map, not the workshop half. That is my own fault for not writing the route down, but it's also the one navigational aid this UI doesn't give: exits are labelled with the *adjacent* room, which is excellent one step at a time and useless for planning a five-room walk. There is no map button, and the `look` button only re-describes where I already am.

It is a genuinely delightful piece of design - the companion who is more useful than he looks - and it is also the single most luck-dependent thing that has happened in a thousand commands. Nothing told me Floyd had found anything. There was no "Floyd drops something at your feet", no line in the `>>` channel I could point to, no marker on the room. If I had not happened to refill my canteen in that particular room on that particular morning, I would have written the closing report of this playthrough saying the game was unwinnable from score 35. Two full legs of careful, systematic searching found nothing; an errand found everything.

The new card's menu also contained a verb I had never seen in 700 commands: **`29 show to floyd`**. A command channel to Floyd does exist - it just never appeared on any of the objects I had been carrying for the previous two legs, and nothing anywhere advertises it.

#### Turns 670-692: down the red elevator, and a shuttle

`160 slide through slot` in the Lower Elevator: **"Elevator enabled."** `15 push down`, `16 wait for the elevator` (the best button in the game, third sighting), and the door opened on a **Waiting Area** - a concrete platform with benches - and east of that the **Kalamontee Platform**, "Shutul Platform -- Kalamontee Staashun", with a transport standing at it. **+4, score 40.**

Inside is **Shuttle Car Alfie**, a cabin with a control cabin at each end. Each cabin has a slot, a lever with `+` / centre / `-` positions, and a digital display reading 0; the east window shows rails running down a long tunnel, the west window shows a concrete wall, which is a quietly excellent way of telling a click player which end is the front.

`143 slide through slot` with the shuttle access card: **"A recorded voice explains that using the shuttle car during the evening hours requires special authorization."** It was evening. So the shuttle is on a timetable, which is a fair puzzle and well-worded - it tells me both what is wrong and that waiting will fix it.

Getting to morning was less fair. There is no `sleep until morning`; there is no `sleep` at all unless the game has decided you are weary, and `6 diagnose` said I was well-rested. So the only way to reach the next day was to ride the elevator back up, walk thirteen rooms to a dorm bed, `11 get in`, and click `5 wait` fourteen times until the game said "You suddenly realize how tired you were" and skipped the night for me. Fourteen identical clicks, in a game that already ships the exact button this needs.

Waking up dumped my entire inventory on the floor again (friction 28/50), including all four access cards - and this time it also **emptied the canteen I had filled and closed**. Leg 2 lost an open canteen overnight and concluded that closing it was the fix. It is not: a closed, brim-full canteen was empty in the morning, with no message on sleeping, waking or looking. The only reason I found out was that the hunger warning changed its wording from "A drink from your canteen would take care of both" to "You think of the machine in the kitchen" - which is, I should say, a genuinely smart warning that reads my inventory. But it is the only notification that my food supply had evaporated, and it arrived one room short of a twenty-five-command round trip back to the Kitchen.

#### Turns 692-812: the shuttle, and a crash I could not judge

In the morning I slid the shuttle access card again: **"Shuttle controls activated."** `12 push lever` and the car pulled out. The display counts up in fives every turn, and the tunnel teaches you the rules by flying signs past the window: "Limit 45", then at the midpoint "Hafwaa Mark -- Beegin Deeseluraashun", then two signs ringed with blinking red lights reading "15" and "5". That is a genuinely good piece of teaching, and it is entirely readable through the click UI because it all arrives as prose events.

What is not good is that there is no way to act on it accurately. The acceleration took nine `4 wait` clicks; I centred the lever at 45 and cruised; the halfway sign went by and I pulled the lever down one turn later than I should have. The car "rumbles through the station and smashes into the wall at the far end", throwing me into the control panel. I survived, but I have no idea what a clean arrival is worth, because the only instrument is a number that changes once per click and the only control is a three-position lever. And note what the shuttle does **not** have: the `wait for the elevator` button. Two elevators in this game collapse "click wait until something happens" into one intention-shaped click. The shuttle, which needs twenty-four waits across one journey, does not.

Out on the **Lawanda Platform** (+4, score 44) the second half of the game opens up, and it is large: an escalator, a Fork, Systems Corridor West/Centre/East, an **Infirmary**, **Planetary Defense**, **Course Control**, a **Library Lobby** and **Library**, **Booth 3**, a **Project Corridor**, a **ProjCon Office**, a **Computer Room**, the **Main Lab** with radiation-lock and bio-lock doors, and **Lab Storage**.

#### Turns 816-877: everything legs 1 and 2 were missing was over here

The Infirmary held a bottle of "Dizeez supreshun medisin -- eksperimentul". Its contents offer only `examine` and `taste`, and `163 taste` answers "The bottle is closed" - so the sequence is `161 open`, at which point an **`165 eat`** appears on the contents and the fever eases. Three clicks and one misleading verb between a dying player and the only medicine in the game.

Taking the bottle triggered the leg's best moment of writing: Floyd finds a bent, rusting robot breast plate in the corner, stares at it in silence, starts sobbing and runs out of the room, and you read the name **Lazarus** engraved on it. That is the payoff for 500 turns of idle chatter, and it fires off a plain `take`.

Then **Lab Storage**, off the Main Lab, which is where the whole game had been hiding:

- a **fresh laser battery**;
- a lab uniform whose pocket, on `13 look inside`, contains a **teleportation access card** and a **piece of paper**;
- and the piece of paper reads: **"Week uv 14-Juun--2882. Kombinaashun tuu Konfurins Ruum: 982."**

So the combination that legs 1 and 2 burned dozens of commands on is written on a scrap of paper in a cupboard on the *other island*, behind the lower elevator, behind the shuttle, behind a card that only exists because Floyd fetched it. Legs 1 and 2 were not being dim; the clue was simply not in the world they could reach. I would call that fair in a typed game with a notebook and hours to spare. In click mode, where every wall is total and there is no way to poke at anything, spending 400 commands in front of a 1001-value dial whose clue is four locks away is a brutal shape.

The teleportation access card, slid in **Booth 3**, answers "Redee." and the brown/beige/tan buttons then move you between Booth 1, 2 and 3 in a single click. Booth 1, I found later, is behind the combination door. The teleport network turns a twelve-move corridor slog into one button, and it is the single biggest quality-of-life change in the game - which makes it a little sad that it is gated behind the last third of the map.

I also swapped the laser's old battery for the fresh one, which took three non-obvious steps: the laser has no `put in…` at all while it is occupied, so you must first click the old battery's own `drop` (which ejects it from the laser), at which point **`172 put in…`** appears on the laser. With a fresh battery the laser still "emits a narrow red beam of light" and still lights nothing, and still has no `shoot at…`.

#### Turns 905-950: 982, and a system repaired

`14 982` on the Rec Area dial: **"The door swings open, and the dial resets to 0."** Behind it is the **Conference Room** and **Booth 1**. (Small thing: setting the correct number opens the door by itself, so my follow-up `2 open door` click got "I don't know the word 2" - the option list had already re-drawn.)

Course Control's blinking light reads "Bedistur Faalyur!", and in the corner is a metal cube containing a **fused ninety-ohm bedistor** - and I had known since leg 1 that a **good ninety-ohm bedistor** sits in a cardboard box in Storage East, on the other island. `18 take` gives "It seems to be fused to its socket"; fetching the **wide-nosed pliers** from the Tool Room grew **`19 take with pliers`** on it, exactly the contextual-verb pattern that is this UI at its best; and `16 put in…` with the good bedistor gave "Done. The warning lights go out and another light goes on." **+6, score 50.** PLANATEREE KORS KUNTROOL fixed; the remaining light now reads "Kors diivurjins minimiizeeng."

Two round trips, Lawanda to Kalamontee and back, which would have been ninety moves a leg ago, cost about a dozen clicks each because of the teleport booths.

#### Turns 956-990: the Computer, the mural, and Floyd

The **ProjCon Office** has a garish mural that "seems to ripple now and then, as though a breeze were blowing behind it", and Floyd says "I don't remember seeing this before". The mural's entire menu is **`10 examine`**. There is no `look behind`, no `move`, no `go south`. The game paints a flashing arrow at a secret and then hands the mouse player nothing to click. Of everything in this playthrough, that is the clearest single example of the click UI failing the fiction.

The **Computer Room**'s pile of output ends: "*** ALURT! ALURT! *** Malfunkshun in Sekshun 384! Sumuneeng reepaar roobot." - and south of it is a **Miniaturization Booth** with a card slot and a numeric keyboard.

And in the **Bio Lock East**, `3 examine lab door` produced the sequence the whole game has been building to. Floyd looks through the window, says "Looks dangerous in there... We'll need card there to fix computer... You open the door, then Floyd will rush in. Then you close door. When Floyd knocks, open door again. Okay? Go!" I opened the door; monsters swarmed; I closed it; a metallic scream; three knocks; I opened it and Floyd stumbled out with the mini-booth card; I closed it again, and Floyd died in my lap while the game sang the Ballad of the Starcrossed Miner. **+2, and +1 for the card: score 53.**

I want to record that this is the one long sequence in the entire game where the click UI was *perfect*. Every step I needed was on screen at the moment I needed it - `15 close lab door` even arrives marked **(new)** - and Floyd states the plan in plain words beforehand, so the sequence plays as intention rather than guesswork. It is also the only time a character asks the player to do something and the interface has the button for it. Compare the 500 turns of "Want to play Hider-and-Seeker?" with no `yes` anywhere.

#### Turns 982-1000: the last two walls

`175 slide through slot` in the Miniaturization Booth: "Miniaturization and teleportation booth activated. Please type in damaged sector number." `11 384`, from the printout: **"Internal computer repair booth not activated."** Another activation gate, and I could not find what activates it.

The other thread ends the same way. Planetary Defense's light reads "Surkit Boord Faalyur -- xis boord kuntroolz xe diskriminaashun surkits", and its access panel holds four identical seventeen-centimetre fromitz boards. `24 take` on the second one slid it out - and the moment it was in my hands the game renamed it a **fried** seventeen-centimetre fromitz board. So the diagnosis is pull-one-and-look, which is fine, but the only spare board in the world is the **cracked** one in Storage East, and I ran out of commands before I could test whether a cracked board beats a fried one.

One self-inflicted wound on the way out, worth logging because the interface invited it: leaving the Bio Lock I clicked `9` for "go southwest" from the room I had just left, and in *this* room 9 was **`drop all`**. My entire inventory - four access cards, the mini card, the teleport card - hit the floor of a sterilisation chamber. The numbers really are per-room, exactly as documented; but a bare `drop all` sitting in the same button row as `look`, `wait` and `diagnose`, with no confirmation and no undo, is a landmine in a UI whose whole interaction model is "click the number you remember".

I stopped at **turn 1000, score 53**, in the Lawanda Infirmary, saved.

### Leg 3 handover (for a hypothetical leg 4)

**Where:** Lawanda Infirmary, night of Septem 9, turn 1000, **score 53**, saved there. Weary, feverish (stage two), fed, canteen empty - the Kitchen is on the other island, two teleport hops away.

**What changed this leg:** three new cards (lower elevator, teleportation, miniaturization) plus the shuttle card now works; the whole **Lawanda complex** open; the **teleport network live** (Booth 1 Conference Room / Booth 2 Elevator Lobby / Booth 3 Library Lobby - slide the teleport card in any booth, then push the button for the booth you want); the **Conference Room** open (combination **982**); **PLANATEREE KORS KUNTROOL repaired** (+6); the Floyd bio-lock sequence completed; Floyd dead.

**The three live leads:**
1. **Planetary Defense** - the second seventeen-centimetre fromitz board is fried and is in my hands; the panel has an empty socket. Try the **cracked** board from the cardboard box in Storage East (Kalamontee). If a cracked board will not do, a good one must be somewhere unvisited - the **radiation-lock door** northeast of the Main Lab, the **Fork's southeast branch**, the **stairway down** from Systems Corridor West, and the **west end of Project Corridor** are all unexplored.
2. **Miniaturization Booth** - card slid, sector number known (**384**), but "internal computer repair booth not activated". Probably one system repair away.
3. **The mural** in the ProjCon Office ripples and Floyd had never seen it. There is no verb for it anywhere. If a leg 4 finds one, that is where I would look next.

**Still unreachable:** both dark rooms (the helicopter manual on the green spool, read in the Library microfilm reader, says the **Helicopter Access Card and Control Panel Key** - the "orange key" - come from "Tranzportaashun Stoorij", i.e. the pitch-black Transportation Supply), the helicopter itself, and the Bio Lab.

**Practical:** the disease kills in "eight to ten days" after the first symptoms (red spool, Library microfilm reader); the Infirmary bottle is empty, so find more medicine. Food is only at the Kalamontee Kitchen. Sleep needs a bed; the Infirmary has some. Beware `drop all` sharing a button row with `look`.

### Report

Across three legs and 1,212 commands this blind, mouse-only run went from the deck of the S.P.S. Feinstein to the Lawanda computer room, finishing at **turn 1000 with a score of 53**. Two deaths, both by starvation, both in leg 1, both recovered by restore; one further restore in leg 1 to undo a magnet that had wiped a run-critical access card. We repaired two of the four broken planetary systems (communications, planetary course control), opened both complexes, activated the teleport network, cracked the combination door, and played the Floyd sacrifice through to its end. We did not win, and we never found the light source that two of the original five walls depend on.

**What the briefing and the opening taught us - and got wrong.** The first sentence of the game says you are scrubbing a deck, and the interface cannot scrub: the brush's menu is examine/drop and there is no floor object to point it at. That is small, but it sets a bad expectation on turn zero - it teaches a click player that the prose and the buttons are not quite the same world. The opening's other lessons are much better. Blather pins you to your post, the ambassador arrives if you `wait`, the explosion opens the pod, and the option list visibly tracks all of it: `open escape pod bulkhead` disappears the moment the door opens, and a `slime` tag appears where the ambassador dripped. The pod descent, the sinking, and the swim out are the best-taught ten minutes in the game.

**The puzzles that worked.** The ladder across the rift is the model: every failed step names the next one ("You couldn't possibly extend the ladder while you're holding it"), and `put across rift` only appears once the ladder is extended and lying on the floor. The coolant repair is the model for cross-room reasoning: seven coloured lights on an enunciator in one room, the same seven colours as buttons on a dispenser in another, both fully legible from the option lists, no free text needed. The magnet growing `take with magnet` on the key in the crevice, and the pliers growing `take with pliers` on the fused bedistor, are the best thing this interface does - the world adds exactly the right verb to exactly the right object at exactly the right moment. The shuttle tunnel teaches its own rules with signs. And the Floyd bio-lock sequence is flawless: the character explains the plan in words, and every click it needs is on screen when it is needed, one of them marked `(new)`.

**The puzzles that did not work, in click mode.** Three tools have no verb that can reach their target: the **oil can** (menu: examine, drop - carried into a rust-covered helicopter, nothing appears), the **laser** (`shoot` is a bare verb with no `shoot at…`, though `throw at…`, `put in…` and `ask about…` all open target lists, and a fresh battery changes nothing), and the **acid** (contents menu: examine, drop; the only pour in the game is the hard-coded funnel in the Comm Room). A typed player can try "oil the rotor" and be told no; we could never form the attempt, so we could never distinguish a wrong idea from a missing button. Two **dark rooms** reduce the entire screen to the exit you came in by - no objects, no other exits, not even a `go down` to gamble on - and Floyd will not follow you in, which also closes the one x-ray a click player has (his `ask about…` list is built from things in scope). The **combination door** sat in front of us for 700 commands with its clue on a scrap of paper on the other island behind three further locks. And the **mural** ripples at you with no verb at all.

**What confused us most.** Not any puzzle - the *withdrawn option*. Ten separate times across the run, a number we had just been shown stopped meaning anything and the game answered **"That is not one of the options on screen (I don't know the word \"106\".)"**. It happened when a container filled up, when a board moved from the room into our hands, when `wait for the elevator` vanished because the car had arrived, when Floyd would not follow us into the dark, when a dial opened its own door, and - worst - on the access cards after a night's sleep. The message accuses a mouse-only player of a typing mistake they are structurally incapable of making, at exactly the moments when something has silently changed underneath them. Close behind it is the class of **silent state change**: the magnet that smears your card one turn after a warning delivered in the same grey as Floyd's small talk; the canteen that empties overnight whether open or closed, with no line of text anywhere; the elevator authorisation that lapses between trips; the `take` that throws a run-critical card on the floor and does not even advance the turn counter.

**What we would change.** Five things, in order. (1) Never answer a withdrawn option with an unknown-word error - say what changed. (2) Give every two-object action a target list, including `shoot at…`, `pour on…` and `oil…`, even if most targets refuse; the refusals are the teaching. (3) Surface health in the clickable layer - weariness already grows a `sleep` button, while hunger, which actually kills, grows nothing, and neither does the disease. (4) Generalise `wait for the elevator`, the best idea in the game, to `sleep until morning`, to the shuttle, and ideally to `go to <known room>`; walking is the single largest cost of the whole playthrough and the design already knows how to fix it. (5) Announce what Floyd does. The companion who wanders off and fetches the card that unlocks the second half of the game is a lovely mechanic that we found **by accident**, some 180 commands after he must have dropped it, while refilling a canteen. One line - "Floyd drops something shiny at your feet" - is the difference between this run ending at 35 and ending at 53.

### Verdict

As a mouse-only experience this is two games stitched together. When the option list is doing its job - growing `take with magnet` on the key, `put across rift` on the extended ladder, `wait for the elevator` while the car is moving, `close lab door` the instant Floyd needs it - it is better than typing: the intention is right there, one click, no vocabulary hunting, and the screen reads as a live model of the world rather than a menu. Those stretches are genuinely excellent, and the Floyd sequence is as good as point-and-click adventure design gets.

But the game is gated by hard locks, and for several of them the click layer offers the player literally nothing: a 1001-value dial, two rooms that collapse the screen to one exit, three tools whose only plausible targets have no verb, and a rippling mural you may only look at. A typed player is never truly stuck in those places - they can flail, and flailing produces information. A mouse player stares at a screen with nothing on it and has to walk away. We got past the wall in the end, and we got past it because a robot we had no way to give an order to happened to drop a card in a room we happened to revisit.

Worth playing, and not yet finishable by clicking alone without luck.
