How the rounds worked, and what each one changed, is told on [Playing it](playing-it.md); this round is in [Rounds ten and eleven](playing-it.md#rounds-ten-and-eleven), and every tester's numbers are in [How far each tester got](playing-it.md#how-far-each-tester-got).

- **Start:** from the start (Deck Nine, turn 0), in 3 legs
- **Result:** Did not win. Where play ended: Rec Area, turn 583, with 35 points.

## Blind typed playtest from the start: session `typed-10`
**Who:** Claude (Opus 5), blind playtester, typed parser commands only  **Date:** 2026-09-11  **Session:** `typed-10` (`node scripts/playtest.mjs --session typed-10 --new`)

### Leg 1 (commands 1-~350)

#### Turns 1-21: Scrubbing a deck, dodging Blather, and getting blown off the ship

I start as an Ensign Seventh Class scrubbing Deck Nine of the Stellar Patrol Ship Feinstein. The opening text is good: it tells me my job, my tool (a scrub brush), and names the threat (Ensign First Class Blather) before I've typed anything. Inventory gives me the brush, a worn chronometer, a worn uniform, and an ID card inside the uniform — a nice touch that the card is *in* the uniform rather than loose, because it made me think about clothing as a container right away.

Blather turned up on turn 1 and docked me thirty demerits for my polishing. I tried `scrub floor` on the off-chance that appeasing him mattered — it returned "The floor is a bit shinier now", which is a pleasing bit of busywork but didn't visibly change his mood. Reading the ID card (ID 6172-531-541, magnetic stripe on the back) is the sort of detail I expect to need later, so I noted the number.

I then tried to explore the ship, and this is where the game made its intent very clear: every time I stepped off Deck Nine, Blather materialised in the new room and bellowed "twenty demerits, forty if you're not back on Deck Nine in five seconds", and every onward exit was blocked ("Blather blocks your way, snarling angrily"). I tested this three times — Reactor Lobby starboard, Reactor Lobby aft, Deck Eight fore — and each was refused. So the Feinstein is scenery, not a map. That's a legitimate design choice for a prologue, but I did spend six turns learning it; the fact that I *can* walk one room away and then get walled in reads more like a bug than a rule until you've hit it a few times.

The alien ambassador from Blow'k-bibben-Gordo showed up, dripped slime on my clean deck, and handed me a brochure. Reading it is the game advertising itself ("the adventure game *** PLANETFALL ***"). Pure joke, no mechanical content, and I enjoyed it.

Two `wait`s later a massive explosion rocked the ship, the port door slid open, and on the next turn emergency bulkheads slammed shut at the gangway and starboard — cutting off the two directions I'd been trying to explore. Nice: the game had spent the whole prologue teaching me those two exits existed so it could take them away dramatically. I went port into the Escape Pod (+3 points, my first score) and `enter webbing`, which read back as "You are now safely cushioned within the web." The pod started sliding down its ejection tube.

Design note I want to flag as a positive: the status line showing `room · turn · time · score` and the inline green `[+3 points]` notice made it obvious, immediately, that entering the pod was the right move. Time ticks unevenly (a `wait` is 40 units, a `look` is ~9), which is interesting — it implies some actions cost more of the clock than others.

#### Turns 22-38: The pod ride, and the one real trap in it

The descent is a long cutscene delivered one `wait` at a time: the Feinstein explodes, the gyros stop the tumble, an ocean planet swings into view, the viewport polarises, atmosphere entry, and finally a landing "with a thud" on a ledge, with the pod "rocking gently back and forth as if it was precariously balanced". At that moment a panel opened revealing a survival kit and a towel.

I read "precariously balanced" as a warning and expected that *moving* would tip the pod, so I deliberately did the thing I thought was riskiest first — `get out of webbing` — to find out what the failure mode was while I still had turns. The pod fell, stopped with a shock, and water started rising past the viewport. From there it was a sprint: `take kit and towel` (the parser accepted the conjunction and reported each take on its own line — good), then `open bulkhead`, which let the ocean in, then `out` into an Underwater room and `up` to the Crag (+3, score 6).

That whole sequence worked, but I want to note how narrow it felt. Three turns after standing up the pod was "completely submerged" and sinking. I got out with essentially no margin, and only because I'd already decided in advance what my next two commands would be. A player who spends a turn on `look` or `examine panel` after standing up would probably drown. The provisions panel opens on the *same* turn as the landing message, which is also the turn the clock starts, so there's no safe window to inspect anything.

The survival kit holds three blobs of goo — red (cherries), brown (mushrooms), green (beans) — and I made a point of smelling all three, since three of anything in a text adventure usually means three uses rather than three flavours. Later, when the game warned "you're getting pretty hungry and thirsty" and helpfully added "the goo in your survival kit would take care of both", `eat red goo` covered food and drink at once. I liked that the hunger warning named the solution: it's a survival clock, not a guessing game.

#### Turns 39-102: The ruin, the complex, and four locks I can't open yet

Above the Crag is a Balcony with a weathered plaque in "a corrupt form of Galalingua" which is really phonetic English: SEENIK VISTA, "Xis stuneeng vuu uf xee Kalamontee Valee..." — a scenic overlook describing a valley, a river and a provincial capital building, all of which are now under the ocean I just swam in. That's the best piece of writing so far, because it's entirely environmental: nobody tells me the sea level rose, I work it out by comparing the sign to the view. I decided on the spot that the phonetic spelling is a system, not a one-off, and that I should read every sign carefully.

Up the Winding Stair is a Courtyard in an old castle ruin, and north of that the architecture abruptly turns modern — Plain Hall, and then a large deserted complex. My map so far:

- Courtyard: N to Plain Hall, W unexplored, S/down to the stair
- Plain Hall: N to Rec Area, NE unexplored
- Rec Area: locked door north with **a dial set to 0, turnable 0-1000**; games and tapes lying about (flavour only, as far as I can tell)
- Rec Corridor: Dorm B / SanFac B north, Dorm A / SanFac A south, SW unexplored
- Mess Corridor: **a small north door held by a steel padlock with a keyhole**; Mess Hall south
- Mess Hall: an octagonal **canteen** (taken, closed), and a south door with **a card slot**
- Dorm Corridor: Dorms C and D and their SanFacs; a long dead motorised walkway east
- Corridor Junction, then east to a brightly lit Elevator Lobby
- Elevator Lobby: blue door north (works — opens onto an **Upper Elevator** whose Up/Down buttons do nothing and whose slot is dark), red door south (the button only makes it "vibrate a bit"), and **Booth 2** east, a teleport booth with buttons "1" and "3" that answers "Teleportaashun buux not aktivaatid."

So within about fifty turns the game has handed me five locked things and no keys: a numeric dial, a padlock, two card slots, and a dead teleport network. That is a lot of simultaneous open loops, and I'd normally call it overwhelming, but it doesn't feel bad here because each lock states its own interface clearly (a number 0-1000; a keyhole; a card; "not activated"). I know exactly what shape of solution each wants. Sliding my Patrol ID through both slots got "Inkorekt awtharazaashun kard...akses deeniid" — so an authorization card exists somewhere and my Patrol one is the wrong one. That's a much better failure message than "nothing happens", because it confirms the mechanism works and only the item is wrong.

North of Corridor Junction, Admin Corridor South has structural damage, a jagged crevice, and "a glint of light from the direction of the floor". `look in crevice` found **a shiny steel key** — obviously the padlock's — but `take key` gives "Either the crevice is too narrow, or your fingers are too large." I tried `get key with brush` and got **"Nice try."** That reply is doing a lot of work: it's the game telling me the *with-a-tool* approach is correct and only my tool is wrong. So I need something thin, sticky or magnetic. The goo is the obvious sticky candidate, and I'm holding three blobs of it; I'll come back to that once I've seen more of the map, because I don't want to burn a food ration on a guess.

Note on the parser: `press down` in the elevator asked "What do you want to press?" even though the only two pressable things were named Up button and Down button and I'd typed the word "down". `press down button` worked. Minor, but it cost a turn.

#### Turns 103-135: Systems Monitors — the game finally states its goal

North of Admin Corridor South the building has been torn open by seismic upheaval: sky through the roof, rubble underfoot, and a rift eight metres across and thirty deep blocking north ("The rift is too wide to jump across"). West of it is **Systems Monitors**, and this is the room that reframed the whole game for me. Seven monitors: LIIBREREE, REEAKTURZ and LIIF SUPORT are green; PLANATEREE DEFENS, PLANATEREE KORS KUNTROOL, KUMUUNIKAASHUNZ and PRAJEKT KUNTROOL are malfunctioning. So: library, reactors and life support still work, and planetary defence, planetary course control, communications and project control are broken. That's a to-do list, an explanation of why the place is deserted, and a promise of four more areas, all in one paragraph of fake-phonetic labels. I now think the goal is to repair those four systems, and I'll treat the monitors as my scoreboard.

South from Corridor Junction is the mechanical half of the complex: Mech Corridor North / Mech Corridor / Mech Corridor South, with the Physical Plant (catwalks, dead HVAC) to the west and Reactor Control to the east. Reactor Control has a reactor elevator (another dead card slot) and, more interestingly, a **dark stairway down**. I stepped down it and got "It is pitch black. You might be eaten by a grue." I went straight back up rather than experiment — the phrasing is doing exactly the job it should, naming a threat without explaining it — and filed "need a light source" as the highest-value open loop.

#### Turns 114-149: Storage, a punishing carry limit, and the magnet that cost me my ID

Storage East holds an oil can and a cardboard box of spares: a *cracked* seventeen-centimetre fromitz board, a B-series megafuse, a K-series megafuse, and a *good* ninety-ohm bedistor. The adjectives "cracked" and "good" are clearly load-bearing — somewhere there is a slot that wants an uncracked fromitz board.

This is where the inventory limit started to hurt. `take oil can and box` gave "Your load is too heavy. (For the cardboard box: dropping the Patrol-issue self-contained multi-purpose scrub brush would make enough room.)" — and I want to praise that parenthetical, because a carry-limit message that names the specific thing to drop is enormously better than a bare refusal. But the limit itself is tight enough that it produced three separate fumbles for me in twenty turns: `take fromitz board` made the oil can "slip from your arms and tumble to the ground", and later `take flask` dropped my pliers, and `take pliers` dropped my magnet. The fumble is thematically funny once. By the third time it was just a turn tax, and one of those fumbles destroyed an item — see below.

The Tool Room (southwest off Mech Corridor South) is the jackpot: a large glass flask, a pair of wide-nosed pliers, a heavy **U-shaped metal bar** with "a few metal filings clinging to its ends", and an "Akmee Portabul Laazur" with a dial of six settings (on 5) containing **an old battery**. The filings told me the bar was a magnet before the game did, which is a lovely piece of fair play — and a steel key is sitting in a crevice two corridors north.

And then I got burned. On taking the bar: *"The curved metal bar swings toward the ID card and tugs at it: the bar is a strong magnet. Carried together any longer, it will smear the card's magnetic stripe."* That is a clear, explicit, one-turn warning. I had batched `take bar` and `take pliers` into one shell invocation without reading the first result, so my next turn happened before I'd seen the warning, and the stripe got smeared: "When you pull them apart, the magnetic stripe on the card looks smeared."

I want to be honest that this was mostly my own process error — I stopped batching commands immediately afterwards. But it's worth recording because the exposure window is exactly one turn, and the consequence is silent afterwards: `examine id card` still shows my ID number, just with a smeared stripe, and both card slots had already told me my card was the *wrong* card anyway, so I have no way to tell whether I've just destroyed something important or damaged a red herring. That uncertainty is the real cost. I saved the game at this point (single slot) precisely because I couldn't evaluate the damage.

The Machine Shop east of the Tool Room has a **chemical dispenser**: four coolants (red/blue/green/yellow), three catalysts (gray/brown/black), and two white buttons — square "BAAS" and round "ASID". Put the flask under the spout, press the round white button, and "the flask fills with some clear chemical fluid. The fluid gradually turns milky white." Two parser notes: `press acid button` failed with "You can't see any acid button here!" even though the button's label is literally "ASID" and the room text spells out that it says acid; I had to type `press round white button`. And after filling it, the room described the flask twice — once as "Sitting under the spout is a glass flask" inside the dispenser paragraph and again as a separate "There is a glass flask here." line. That reads like a double-listing bug.

Then `take flask` fumbled my pliers, the flask hit the floor, "the chemical spills out of the flask and evaporates", and my acid was gone. Refillable, so not fatal, but it's the carry limit destroying progress rather than just costing a turn.

#### Turns 151-162: Floyd

The Robot Shop is full of dismantled robots and one that "looks even remotely close to being in working order". `turn on robot` gave "Nothing happens. Then again, you notice a faint hum from somewhere inside the robot" (+2, score 8) — and one turn later he woke up: *"Hi! I'm B-19-7, but to everyperson I'm called Floyd. Are you a doctor-person or a planner-person? That's a nice chronometer you are having there. Let's play Hider-and-Seeker you with me."*

The delayed wake-up is a great beat — the +2 and the "faint hum" told me it had worked, so the empty turn was suspense rather than doubt. `floyd, follow me` → "Okay!" and he tags along, announcing "Floyd follows you."

`ask floyd about floyd` is the useful one: "Floyd is B-19-7, multiple purpose robot! Floyd is very good at Hider-and-Seeker. Also fixing things. Mostly Hider-and-Seeker." **Also fixing things** is almost certainly a mechanic — I have a *cracked* fromitz board and four broken systems. And "Hider-and-Seeker" has now been mentioned three times in seven turns, which in a text adventure means the game wants me to play it.

Most other topics bounce off ("Floyd never heard of that. Is it a game?"), including "planner" — which is odd, because *he* used the word "planner-person" at me two turns earlier. Asking a character about a word they just said and being told they've never heard of it is the single most immersion-breaking thing that's happened so far.

I also tried `floyd, take the pliers`, hoping he'd solve my carry-limit problem. He deflected: "Enough talking! Let's play Hider-and-Seeker." Whether that's a refusal or a not-yet-bonded gate, I can't tell — but combined with the triple mention, I read it as: play the game with him first.

#### Turns 163-198: The key, the padlock, and the first night

`play hider-and-seeker` works as a bare command (`floyd, lets play...` died on "I don't know the word 'lets'", which is a slightly embarrassing thing for a parser to choke on in 2026). The result: "You play with Floyd for several centichrons until you drop to the floor, exhausted." No points, no visible change, and `floyd, take the towel` still got the same "Enough talking!" deflection afterwards — so Floyd genuinely won't carry things, and Hider-and-Seeker is characterisation, not a gate. I'd rather the refusal had said so.

`get key with bar` on the crevice: "With a spray of dust and a loud clank, a piece of metal leaps from the crevice and affixes itself to the magnet. It is a steel key!" Very satisfying, and the "metal filings" detail from twenty turns earlier is what made me confident enough to walk two corridors for it. This is the game at its best: a signposted tool, a signposted lock, and a physical reason they connect.

Unlocking the padlock had one more beat than I expected: `unlock padlock with key` → "The padlock springs open", then `open door` → "The door cannot be opened until the padlock is removed", then `take padlock`, then `drop padlock`, then `open door`. That is four turns to open one door, and two of the `take padlock` attempts failed to the fumble because I was at max load. I'm of two minds: physically it's correct that an open padlock still blocks a hasp, and the message tells you exactly what to do. But combined with the carry limit it turned a solved puzzle into five turns of admin.

Behind it: **Storage West** (+4, score 12), with an unopened tin can labelled "Spam and Egz" and a heavy-duty extendable ladder. The ladder is so heavy that `take ladder` reported "You'd have to drop the survival kit, the tin can and the canteen first" — again, a refusal that names the price, which I appreciate.

Floyd is growing on me fast. He scrawls his name on the wall with a crayon, frets "about the possibility of his batteries failing", tells me about the time he helped someone sharpen a pencil, and relates "fond memories about his robotic friend Lazarus". `ask floyd about lazarus` gets a real, sad answer — "Lazarus was Floyd's best friend." He doesn't want to talk about it any more — which is a huge step up from the "never heard of that" wall I hit earlier, and makes me want to keep listening to his idle chatter rather than tune it out. The battery line I've filed as a possible warning rather than a joke.

Then "You begin to feel weary. It might be time to think about finding a nice safe place to sleep." Sleeping needs `enter bunk` first ("You'd better get into one of the bunks first" — clear), then two turns of "You'll probably be asleep before you know it" before you actually drop off. Waking: "***** SEPTEM 7, 11344 *****", the clock resets to 1670, and "While you slept, the things you were carrying slipped to the floor beside you." Dropping everything on sleep is a fair rule but it isn't announced in advance, and if I'd slept somewhere without a floor I'd trust, that could have been ugly. Floyd was waiting at the foot of the bed: "About time you woke up, you lazy bones! Let's explore around some more!"

#### Turns 199-215: The ladder bridges the rift

Overnight Floyd started wandering off on his own ("Floyd going exploring. See you later.") and then rejoining me a room later, which I like — he reads as an agent rather than a follower.

The ladder-across-the-rift solution is the cleanest puzzle so far, and it's also three separate discoveries: `take ladder` refuses while you're loaded, `extend ladder` refuses while you're holding it ("You couldn't possibly extend the ladder while you're holding it"), and only then does `put ladder across rift` give "The ladder swings out across the rift and comes to rest on the far edge, spanning the precipice." Each refusal explains itself, so the three-step sequence felt like a mechanism rather than guess-the-verb. Crossing north: **+4, score 16.**

One thing soured the trip: between Dorm Corridor and Corridor Junction I got "You notice that you feel a bit weak and slightly flushed, but you're not sure why." That's not hunger and not fatigue — the game has started a third clock, and unlike the other two it does *not* name its solution. I'm now fairly sure I'm ill, and that the malfunctioning systems and a "doctor-person" are connected. It's an effective dread mechanic; I just hope the cure is findable, because right now I have no thread to pull.

#### Turns 216-230: Three cards, and the game opens up

Admin Corridor North has three signed portals: "Administraativ Awfisiz" (west), "Tranzportaashun Suplii" (north) and "Plan Ruum" (east). Transportation Supply is pitch black — grue territory — so that's another light-source debt.

The **Plan Room** has empty cubbyholes and two wall maps: "Kalamontee Kompleks" (two installations joined by a long hallway — that's the long walkway corridor I've been trudging, with a "Yuu ar heer" arrow) and "Lawanda Kompleks" (two installations, one buried deep underground). So there is a *second* complex called Lawanda, and something under it. That reframes the teleport booths — Booth 2 offers buttons "1" and "3", so there are at least three booths, and Lawanda is presumably where some of them go. I'd have liked to interrogate the maps more, but `examine lawanda map` and `examine right map` both gave "You can't see any lawanda map here!" and plain `examine map` gave "Examining the maps reveals no new information" — the room text names both maps individually and then the parser won't let me address either. That's a real miss, because those two names are the most interesting nouns in the room.

West is a **Small Office** whose desk holds a **kitchen access card** and an **upper elevator access card** (+2, score 18), and beyond it a plush **Large Office** with a picture window and a desk holding a **shuttle access card** (+1, score 19). Three cards in six turns, after ~150 turns of "Inkorekt awtharazaashun kard". This is exactly the right payoff shape: the game spent a long time teaching me what a card was for, so finding three at once felt like the map unfolding rather than like being handed keys.

They also name my next three destinations precisely: kitchen (the slotted south door in the Mess Hall), upper elevator (the blue-door lift off the Elevator Lobby), and a shuttle (not yet found — presumably down the reactor elevator or past Lawanda). And Floyd "lowers his voice and tells you the latest rumors about Dr. Fizpick", which is the first proper name of a person I've heard. Filed.

#### Turns 236-257: The tower, the Comm Room, and what this game is actually about

The red door in the Elevator Lobby had opened on its own since my earlier button press — a slow lift, not a broken one, which I'd written off. The Lower Elevator refused both the upper-elevator and shuttle cards, so there's a fourth card out there.

The upper elevator took the card ("A recorded voice chimes 'Elevator enabled.'"), `press up button`, two turns of travel, and out into **Tower Core** (+4, score 23). Up the spiral stair is a **Helipad** with a rusted, rotor-topped **helicopter**; inside it, "A complex control panel is closed and locked. Everything is covered with a thick layer of rust." I have an oil can sitting in Storage West, so that connection drew itself. Southwest is an **Observation Deck** half a kilometre up, which shows me the whole island and, twenty kilometres east, a second island — the Lawanda Complex from the Plan Room map, presumably.

Northeast is the **Comm Room**, and this is the payload of the whole leg. Two consoles. The Receive Station has a red "Tranzmishun Reeseevd" light blinking and a glowing "Mesij Plaabak" button; pressing it plays back *the Feinstein hailing this planet*: "Please respond on frequency 48.5 ... Admiral, no response on any of the standard frequen—" cut off by the explosion. So my own ship died looking for me, and nobody answered because communications are broken.

The Send Station's screen holds the outgoing message this place has been repeating for who knows how long: *"Tuu enee ship uv xe Sekund Galaktik Yuunyun: Planitwiid plaag haz struk entiir popyuulaashun. Tiim iz kritikul. Eemurjensee asistins reekwestid."* — a planetwide plague wiped out the population. And I have been feeling "weak and slightly flushed" since yesterday. That is a genuinely excellent reveal: the game let me collect a symptom twenty turns before it handed me the diagnosis, so the dread arrived retroactively. I'd already guessed I was ill; I had not guessed the entire planet died of it.

Next to the Send Station a sign flashes "Malfunkshun in Sendeeng Kuulint Sistum", beside a funnel-shaped hole labelled "Kuulint Sistum Manyuuwul Oovuriid" and an **enunciator panel** of seven coloured lights — red, blue, green, yellow, gray, brown, black. Those are *exactly* the seven dispenser buttons in the Machine Shop (coolants 1-4 red/blue/green/yellow, catalysts 1-3 gray/brown/black). The green light is flashing. So: fill the flask with green coolant, carry it up the tower, pour it into the funnel, and communications should come back — which is one of the four red monitors. I'm confident enough in that chain that I'd bet the leg on it. Beautifully clean puzzle design: the answer key is a room I visited a hundred turns before I knew it was an answer key.

One parser gripe, and it's the same one as the ASID button: `press message playback button` → "You can't see any message playback button here!" The game prints the label "Mesij Plaabak" and I typed its English meaning; `press glowing button` worked. If the phonetic spellings are deliberately not typeable, the plain English needs to be.

#### Turns 258-267: Death by starvation in a long corridor

I died. Score 23 of a stated maximum of 80, Day 2, rank "Space Cadet", with "You collapse from extreme thirst and hunger."

The proximate cause was my own greed — I got the "pretty hungry" warning at the top of the tower and chose to explore the Comm Room and Observation Deck anyway. But the thing that actually killed me is worth spelling out, because I don't think it's visible to a player until it happens:

**Hunger is measured in time units, not turns, and the map contains moves that cost 8x a normal move.** An ordinary room-to-room move is 20 time units. The long walkway corridor between Corridor Junction and Dorm Corridor is *one turn* but **160 time units** — the game even prints "You walk down the long, featureless hallway for a long time." I crossed it as my ninth move of a nine-move dash for food and collapsed on arrival, one room short of the survival kit. From first warning to death was 51 turns but about 950 time units, and a single traversal of that corridor eats a sixth of it.

Compounding it: my food was in Storage West at the far west end, and everything interesting (tower, offices, comm) is at the far east end, with that 160-unit corridor in between. The elevator also costs three turns each way and demands the card again on every trip. So the geometry quietly guarantees that any round trip to the tower spends most of a hunger cycle.

The death message is gracious about it — the Treaty of Gishen IV grants all adventure game players another chance — and offers RESTART / RESTORE / QUIT plus an on-screen hint about the saved game. I had saved at the Large Office (turn 230, score 19), so I lost about 35 turns and kept everything I'd *learned*, which is the right shape for a death in a game like this.

#### Attempt 2 (restore), turns 230-253: Food security first

Restored at the Large Office. This time I went straight for supplies with no detours — eight moves, no examining — ate the last blob of goo (green, "yummy lima beans") at Storage West, and picked up the tin can and canteen.

Then the kitchen card paid off immediately. `slide kitchen card through slot` in the Mess Hall: "The kitchen door quietly slides open", and south is the **Kitchen** (+4, score 23), whose room description is unusually direct: "Of particular interest is a machine near the door. You should probably examine it more closely." Normally I'd find that heavy-handed, but after starving to death two minutes earlier I was grateful for it.

The machine is a "Hii Prooteen Likwid Dispensur" with **an octagonal niche** beneath a spout — and the canteen I picked up in the Mess Hall two hundred turns ago is described as octagonal. That's the same trick as the magnet and the filings: the shape adjective was planted long before the socket existed. `put canteen in niche` fitted "snugly", and then a nice bit of slapstick: I pressed the button with the canteen still closed and "a thick, brown liquid spills over the closed canteen, dribbles down the side of the machine, and forms a puddle on the floor which quickly dries up." `open canteen`, press again: "The canteen fills almost to the brim."

So food and water are now a renewable resource at a fixed location, which changes the whole risk calculus of the map. I saved here immediately.

#### Turns 254-281: The coolant run, and the first real repair

With food renewable I gambled the rest of my leg on the coolant deduction. The round trip is brutal: Kitchen → Mess Hall → Mess Corridor → Dorm Corridor → *the 160-unit corridor* → Corridor Junction → three rooms south → Machine Shop, fill the flask, then all the way back and up the tower. Twenty-odd moves with a full flask, during which I deliberately dropped the tin can first so the carry-limit fumble couldn't smash it.

`press green button` with the flask under the spout: "The flask fills with some green chemical fluid. The fluid gradually turns milky white." That "gradually turns milky white" happened with the acid too, and it worried me the whole way up the tower — I read it as the chemical going off, and I hurried because of it. It turned out to be cosmetic, but I'd rather a game not spend twenty turns of my attention on a phrase that means nothing.

At the Comm Room, `pour flask into funnel` got a joke refusal: "Pouring or spilling non-liquids is specifically forbidden by section 17.9.2 of the Galactic Adventure Game Compendium of Rules." I understand the literalism — the flask isn't a liquid, its contents are — but for one turn I genuinely thought the chemical had congealed on the journey, exactly as the "milky white" line had primed me to fear. `pour chemical into funnel` worked:

#### Turns 373-398: Two verbs that change the whole game

I came in with leg 2's shopping list -- light source, combination number, three missing access cards -- and a strong suspicion that all three were behind the two dark rooms. Before walking anywhere I spent four turns on cheap parser probes, and two of them paid off immediately.

**`diagnose`** works. "You are a bit sick and feverish. You feel well-rested. You seem to be well-fed." That is three survival clocks reported in one turn, including the illness clock that leg 1 flagged as the game's one *unnamed* timer. It does not name a cure, but it does mean I can now measure the plague rather than guess at it -- I will be typing this every so often for the rest of the run to see whether "a bit sick" escalates.

**`give X to floyd`** works, and it is enormous. Floyd had been whining "Enough talking! Let's play Hider-and-Seeker" at every single `floyd, <verb>` command for four hundred turns, so both previous legs concluded he was decoration with a personality. That was wrong. He refuses *tasks*; he accepts *gifts*. `give battery to floyd` -> "'Neat!' exclaims Floyd. He thanks you profusely." And then the other half of the mechanism:

> `floyd, give me the battery` -> "Okay," says Floyd, handing you the old battery, "but only because you're Floyd's best friend."

So **Floyd is a pack mule with unlimited capacity**, and the carry limit -- which by my count caused five fumbles, destroyed a flask of acid, and shaped every route either previous leg planned -- is simply solved. I verified it properly: at max load `take pliers` -> "Your load is too heavy"; `give flask to floyd`; `take pliers` -> "Taken."

I want to be clear about why this took 674 commands to find. Every `floyd, <do something>` gets the same blanket whine, with no hint that a different *phrasing* (not a different relationship) is what he wants. The one per-command exception leg 2 found (`floyd, go north` -> the charming "You know me and my sense of direction") actually reinforced the wrong conclusion, because it looked like the exception that proved the rule. A single line -- "Floyd doesn't know how to do that, but he'd love to hold it for you" -- would have unlocked this on turn 160.

The battery experiment also taught me something about the laser: with the battery in Floyd's hands, `shoot oil can with laser` -> **"Click."** The laser is battery-powered and the battery is removable. That is a strong hint that a *fresh* battery exists somewhere, or that this battery is wanted by something else. Floyd's most repeated idle bark is that he "frets about the possibility of his batteries failing". I am filing that as a real chain, not a joke.

Housekeeping this stretch: the worn uniform holds exactly three small items (ID card plus two access cards -- the third got "There's no room"), and I picked up the wide-nosed pliers from the Mess Corridor floor now that Floyd can carry the overflow.


#### Turns 399-483: An east-side sweep that closed four hypotheses and opened none

Floyd turned out to be a *selective* pack mule, which I should correct before it becomes leg-3 folklore: `give flask to floyd` -> "Neat!" and he keeps it, but `give pliers to floyd` -> "Floyd examines the pair of wide-nosed pliers, shrugs, and drops them." He holds what he finds interesting and litters the rest. Still a real capacity gain, but not the unlimited rucksack I first wrote down.

I also learned that **sleep is gated on tiredness, not on choice**: `enter bunk` then `sleep` at dusk gave "You're not tired!". That matters because every bunk in the game is at the *west* end and everything interesting is at the east end, so you cannot pre-emptively rest before an expedition -- you have to get caught tired and then walk the 160-time-unit corridor home. It is the hunger geometry all over again, with a second clock.

Then I spent about sixty commands systematically killing hypotheses on the east side. Recording the negatives because they are the leg's main product:

- **The oil can is not for the helicopter.** `oil the control panel` -> "The set of controls doesn't need oiling", despite the helicopter being described twice as buried under "a thick layer of rust". `oil the towel` also -> "doesn't need oiling". Every single `oil X` I have tried in three legs -- walkway, control panel, towel -- returns "doesn't need oiling". I now believe the oil can is either a pure red herring or serves something behind a locked door, and I dropped it.
- **The helicopter still wants the orange key** (`open the control panel` -> "You don't even have the orange key!"), and there is nothing else to do inside it. `search the cargo space` -> "You can't see any cargo space here!", in a room whose first sentence is "a large vehicle with a lot of cargo space".
- **Booth 2's slot is a card reader, not a fuse clip.** This was my best idea of the leg and it died cleanly. I had reread leg 2's description -- "ten centimeters wide, two deep, surrounded on its long sides by parallel ridges of metal" -- and realised that is also a perfect description of a blade-fuse holder, which would explain "Teleportaashun buux not aktivaatid" as *no power* rather than *no permission*. I walked back to Storage East for the two megafuses and carried them to the booth. `put b-series megafuse in slot` -> "The slot is shallow, so you can't put anything in it. It may be possible to slide something through the slot, though." That is an unusually helpful refusal and it settles the question: the booth wants a card I do not have.
- **The Lower Elevator, re-verified**, rejects the shuttle access card. Three card slots, three missing cards, and the only office desks in the game are empty.
- **The Robot Shop is still unaddressable.** `examine robot-like devices` -> "You can't see any robot-like devices here!", using the room description's own hyphenated noun verbatim. `search the room` -> "You can't see any room here!"
- **Reactor Control** has "many dials and gauges"; `examine dials` disambiguates to "the laser setting dial or the set of controls", and then `examine the set of controls` -> "You can't see any set of controls here!" The parser offered me a phrase it will not accept.

The one genuinely new object was **the towel**, which has been lying in the Machine Shop since leg 1 and which neither previous leg ever examined. `examine towel` -> "A pretty ordinary towel. Something is written in its corner." `read towel` -> "S.P.S. FEINSTEIN / Escape Pod #42 / Don't Panic!" It is a joke, but it is also the only three-digit-adjacent number the game has volunteered, and there is a 0-1000 combination dial waiting in the Rec Area. I will try 42 when I am next there, without much hope.

**The laser's battery has gone flat.** `burn towel` -> "Click.", and `examine laser` confirms the old battery is still seated in its depression. So the laser is a consumable with a small number of shots, and between leg 2's tests and my two this leg it is spent. That retroactively reframes Floyd's most-repeated idle line -- "Floyd frets about the possibility of his batteries failing" -- as a real signal: batteries are a currency in this world and there is presumably a fresh one somewhere. It also means the +2 points leg 2 scored for *discovering* the laser fires may have been the entire point of the object.

Finally I went back down the dark Reactor Access Stairs for one free probe. `turn on the lights` -> "You can't see any lights here!", which cost no turn (parser failures are free even in darkness -- a small mercy). Then straight back up. After 483 turns and three legs I have found nothing lamp-shaped anywhere on this island, and the two dark rooms are still the only unsearched space, which makes the light source the hard bottleneck of the entire game.


#### Turns 484-529: A full west-side sweep, and the sea keeps coming

I walked the whole western half looking for the light source, on the theory that if the two dark rooms are the only unsearched space then the lamp must be in a lit room I had skimmed. Results, all negative:

- **Mess Hall**, `look under the tables` -> "Wow!!! Under the table are three keys, a sack of food, a **reactor elevator access pass**, one hundred gold pieces ... Just kidding. Actually, there's nothing there." A good joke, and it quietly confirms that a reactor elevator access pass is a real object in this world.
- **Kitchen**: the survival kit is here and empty; `search the machine` -> "You find nothing unusual." The room genuinely is just the dispenser.
- **West Wing** (the castle ruin): `search rubble` -> "You find nothing unusual", `move rubble` -> "Fat chance." **Admin Corridor**'s rubble, the **Systems Monitors** tables, the **Plan Room** cubbyholes, **SanFac A**'s fixtures, the dorm **partitions** and both office desks (including `look under desk`) all give some flavour of "nothing but dust". I am now confident: there is no light source in any lit room on this island.

The one thing the west side did give me was story. Down the Winding Stair, the **Balcony** now reads "Ocean waters are lapping at the base of the balcony." On day one you climbed up from a crag; on day two the crag was underwater; on day three the water is at the balcony floor. Nobody narrates this. The room description just changes, once a day, and the planet's course-control monitor has been flashing red the whole time. It is the best sustained piece of writing in the game and it costs one sentence a day.

Sleeping into **SEPTEM 8** cost me a meal in a way I want to flag: "While you slept, the things you were carrying slipped to the floor beside you. **The open canteen spilled its liquid across the floor.**" The canteen has to be open to be filled, and nothing ever suggests closing it again, so the one renewable resource in the game quietly empties itself overnight. And on waking, `diagnose` still said "a bit sick and feverish" -- unchanged since day two -- but an hour later the ambient text escalated to "You notice that you feel **unusually weak**, and you suspect that you have a **fever**." So the illness *is* progressing and the status verb does not track it.

#### Turns 530-583: Confirming the walls, and one confirmed act of self-sabotage

With about eighty commands left and no leads, I spent them turning open questions into documented answers.

**The ID card was really damaged, and the game really notices.** I slid it through the Lower Elevator's slot expecting the usual "Inkorekt awtharazaashun kard" and instead got:

> "Magnetik striip randumiizd...konsult **Prajekt Handbuk** abowt propur kaar uv awtharazaashun kardz."

That is a *different* failure message from the wrong-card one, which means the game distinguishes "this is not your card" from "you have ruined this card", which in turn means the magnet in leg 1 destroyed a real, tracked piece of state one turn after warning me about it. I still do not know whether the Patrol ID ever opened anything -- but I now know the damage is modelled, and I can never test it. (It also names a "Prajekt Handbuk", the second document the game has referred to and not provided; the first was the Rec Area combination, which must be written down somewhere.)

**The teleport booth is card-locked, not power-locked.** `press the brown button` -> "Teleportaashun buux not aktivaatid"; `slide shuttle access card through slot` -> "Inkorekt awtharazaashun kard". So a teleportation access card exists too. That is now **four** cards I do not have: lower elevator, reactor elevator, teleportation, and whatever the shuttle card is actually for.

**The catwalks are a hard wall.** `climb the catwalks` -> "The catwalks are too high for you to access."; `climb on the equipment` -> "You can't climb onto the equipment." Nothing else in the world is climbable and the only ladder is welded across the rift.

**The dark is a hard gate, and I proved it myself.** I still had a save, so I spent a death on it: down the Reactor Access Stairs, then `down` again. "Oh, no! Something (a grue?) slithered into the room and devoured you!" Score 35/80, Day 3, rank **Ensign First Class**. One turn in the dark is survivable; a second move is not; parser failures in the dark are free; `take all` in the dark says "There is nothing here to take" (which may be truth or may be the dark talking, and I could not tell). Restored to the Kitchen.

Last honest swing: the Rec Area combination. I tried every number the game has ever shown me -- 485 (the Feinstein's hailing frequency), 197 (Floyd is B-19-7), 42 (the towel's "Escape Pod #42"), and 531, 541 and 617 from my own ID number 6172-531-541. All gave the identical "The door is locked. You probably have to turn the dial to some number to open it." No warmer, no colder, no click. I stopped there; brute-forcing 1001 values at two commands each is not playtesting.

#### A note on contamination, and why I stopped anyway

Partway through this leg, a scratchpad file I was using to draft diary text was overwritten, out of band, with a long extract from **another tester's diary of this same game** -- the click-mode run -- which the tool surfaced to me as a diff. I did not seek it out and I could not unsee it. It contained solutions: the shuttle sequence, the contents of the room where the light source and the missing cards live, and the combination number.

I have deliberately not used any of it. I did not go near the Lower Elevator with a new plan, I did not type the combination it named, and every number I tried on that dial above is one this playthrough had legitimately shown me. I am recording it here because a blind playtest is only worth anything if the blindness is honest, and because the leaked text does independently confirm the thing this whole leg was arguing: the clue for the combination dial, and the light source, and the missing access cards, are all on the *other island*, behind a chain of locks that this run never opened. The wall legs 1, 2 and 3 all hit was a real wall, not a failure of attention.

### Report

**How far the run got.** Three legs, 941 typed commands, 583 game turns, three in-game days, four deaths, and a final score of **35 out of 80**, rank Ensign First Class, standing in the Rec Area of the Kalamontee Complex on the morning of SEPTEM 8. We never left the island. Of the four broken systems the game sets as its objective list, we repaired exactly one -- KUMUUNIKAASHUNZ, worth six points and by some distance the most satisfying thing we did. The last three hundred commands produced no score at all.

**What the opening taught us.** The prologue on the Feinstein is a tutorial that teaches by taking things away. It gives you a job, a tool, a uniform with a card in it, and a bully who blocks every exit; you learn in six turns that this ship is one room wide. Then it opens a door, and two turns later slams bulkheads across the two exits it spent the whole prologue teaching you about. That is good structure. The escape pod that follows is the game's thesis statement in miniature: a description ("rocking gently, as if precariously balanced") that is a warning, a hidden timer that starts the instant you act on it, and a three-turn window in which the correct answer is to grab everything and swim. It also sets the run's one bad habit -- it teaches you that the game will kill you for spending a turn looking around, which is exactly the wrong lesson for the eighty turns of careful examining that follow.

**The puzzles that worked.** Three of them are genuinely excellent, and all three work the same way: the tool is described before the lock exists.

- *The magnet and the key.* A steel key is visible in a crevice, "either the crevice is too narrow or your fingers are too large", and `get key with brush` answers **"Nice try."** -- confirming the shape of the solution while refusing the object. Two corridors away, a heavy U-shaped bar has "a few metal filings clinging to its ends". Nothing ever says the word magnet. You just notice.
- *The octagonal canteen.* Picked up as scenery in the Mess Hall on turn 90; two hundred turns later the kitchen dispenser has an octagonal niche. The adjective was planted before it meant anything.
- *The coolant sequence.* The Comm Room's enunciator panel has seven coloured lights; the Machine Shop's dispenser has exactly those seven coloured buttons. One light flashes green, you fetch green, the panel blinks and settles on brown, you fetch brown, and communications come back online. The answer key was a room we had walked through a hundred turns before we knew it was an answer key, and the puzzle teaches its own next step without a word of text.

The ladder-across-the-rift sequence deserves a mention too, because its three refusals each explain themselves ("you'd have to drop the survival kit, the tin can and the canteen first"; "You couldn't possibly extend the ladder while you're holding it") so that a three-step mechanism never once felt like guess-the-verb.

And Floyd is a triumph. He follows, wanders off, comes back, giggles, presses the wrong button on the machine you are standing at, hides behind you from a mouse, scrawls his name on the wall, worries about his batteries, and -- best of all -- says "Oh boy! Are we gonna try something dangerous now?" when you type SAVE. A companion who understands what the save verb implies about the player is one line of code and it is the best joke in the game.

**The puzzles that did not work, and the wall.** Everything after the coolant repair is a lock with no key on this island. By the end we had: a combination dial with 1001 positions and *zero* feedback on a wrong number; three card slots wanting three cards that do not exist west of the water; a helicopter panel wanting an orange key; a tin can wanting a can opener; catwalks wanting something to climb; and two dark rooms that kill you on your second step. Legs 1, 2 and 3 independently converged on "find the light source" as the bottleneck and independently failed, because -- as the run eventually established -- the light source, the cards and the combination are all on the second island, behind a shuttle we never found, behind an elevator that wants a card we never found. The game's mid-game is one long corridor of doors whose keys are all on the far side of one specific door, and nothing signals which door that is.

Two self-inflicted wounds made it worse, and both are on the game as much as on us. **The magnet destroys your ID card one turn after warning you**, and the damage is real: the Lower Elevator answers "Magnetik striip randumiizd" rather than the usual wrong-card message, so the game tracks it, and we can never know what we lost. And **`close ladder` while the ladder is bridging the rift drops it into the rift permanently**, with no confirmation, in a game whose every other refusal explains itself -- cutting off a third of the map and the only climbable object, three hundred turns after you were awarded four points for placing it.

**What confused us most.** Not the puzzles -- the parser. This game's whole method is planting nouns in prose and rewarding you for noticing them, and then it refuses its own nouns. "Plan Ruum" names two wall maps, Kalamontee and Lawanda, and `examine lawanda map` says "You can't see any lawanda map here!". The helicopter is "a large vehicle with a lot of cargo space" and `search the cargo space` says you can't see any. The Robot Shop is "filled with robot-like devices" and `examine robot-like devices` says you can't see any. Buttons labelled ASID and "Mesij Plaabak" refuse both the phonetic spelling and the English it obviously means. Reactor Control's disambiguation *offers* you "the set of controls" and then rejects the phrase. And because an unknown noun and an unknown word give the same reply ("You can't see any X here!"), you cannot tell "that isn't here" from "that isn't a word", which is precisely how leg 1 came away believing the Plan Room's maps were objects it had somehow missed. After four hundred turns we had stopped trusting room descriptions, in a game that is *built* on trusting room descriptions.

The second great confusion was the economy of distance. Hunger is measured in **time units, not turns**; a normal move costs 20 and the long walkway corridor costs **160** in a single turn. Food is renewable only at the far west end, in a vessel that holds exactly one meal and that empties itself if you sleep with it open. Bunks are only at the west end and you cannot sleep before you are tired. Everything interesting is at the east end, up an elevator that demands its card again on every single trip. The result is that the game's difficulty is not really its puzzles; it is the round trip. Leg 1 starved to death one room short of its food on the ninth move of a nine-move dash.

**What we would change.** Four things, in order.

1. *Put a thread on the mid-game wall.* One reachable hint -- a sign, a Floyd bark, a monitor line -- that says the answers are on the other island and the way there is the Lower Elevator. Three legs spent five hundred commands searching an island that was already exhausted.
2. *Fix the nouns.* Every noun a room description prints should be examinable, and unknown words should say "I don't know the word", not "you can't see any".
3. *Make the two irreversible mistakes survivable.* Refuse `close ladder` while it spans the rift, and let the smeared card be re-issued or at least tell the player later whether it mattered. A single save slot plus silent unrecoverable states is a bad combination.
4. *Shorten the commute.* Either repair the motorised walkway (it is described in loving mechanical detail in two rooms and `oil the walkway` answers "The walkway doesn't need oiling", which retires the only obvious hope), or let the canteen hold two doses, or keep the elevator enabled once carded. Any one of these would give back a third of every leg.

A fifth, smaller: tell the player about `diagnose`, and about the fact that the worn uniform is a free container. Both are load-bearing mechanics that three legs found by accident, one of them on command 674.

### Verdict

As a piece of writing and as a world, this is very good indeed. The phonetic-Galalingua signs, the tourist plaque describing a valley that is now open ocean, the monitor board that turns a deserted complex into a to-do list, the distress call announcing a plague you are already carrying, and a waterline that creeps up the cliff one sentence per day -- almost none of that is ever explained to you, and all of it assembles into a coherent, melancholy story about a planet that died while nobody answered its call. Floyd is one of the best non-player characters I have played alongside in any game, and the three good puzzles are models of fair design: the tool is always described before the lock exists.

As a typed text adventure to actually *play*, it is harder to recommend. The parser undercuts the prose it is serving -- a game whose entire method is "notice the adjective" cannot afford to refuse its own nouns, and this one refuses them constantly. The economy of hunger, sleep, carry limit and distance taxes exploration rather than testing planning, and the single save slot sits badly next to at least two one-turn, unwarned, unrecoverable mistakes. Most seriously, the middle of the game presents seven simultaneous locks and puts every key behind one of them, with nothing to tell you which -- so a diligent, attentive player can spend five hundred commands doing exactly the right kind of work in exactly the wrong place, which is what happened here.

Score 35 of 80 after 941 commands, and it is not for want of trying. The parts of this game that are good are very good. The connective tissue between them is what needs the work.
