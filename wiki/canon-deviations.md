# Where we left the original

This remake is a hand port of Infocom's 1983 *Planetfall*. Its first rule was to copy the original's routines and text word for word. Its second rule, set by the user on 10 September at 12:49, allowed exceptions: ***a little common-sense departure from the source was fine, and the port need not stay perfectly true where gameplay would be much better for it***. This page lists every deliberate deviation in one place: what the original does, what this port does instead, why, and who decided. On 23 September ***the user asked for a whole article on the deviations made so far***.

## How a deviation is made and marked

A deviation starts as a problem that a player ran into. Most came from the blind playtest rounds of 10 to 18 September (see [Playing it](playing-it.md)), and the rest from the user's own play and reviews. Norm sorts each round's findings into three kinds: A, a fix that is faithful or only changes the interface; B, a departure from the original, which needs the user's approval; and C, leave it as the original. Then Norm waits for the user. Every B that is approved is marked in the code with a comment that begins `Deliberate deviation (user decision, <date>, <round>)` and says what the source does and why this differs (HANDOFF.md 460-461). There are about ninety such comments in the engine, the rule modules and the page: 25 dated 10 September, 32 dated 11 September, 17 dated 12 September, and a few on each day from 16 to 18 September. Most have a test that pins them.

Three kinds of change are **not** deviations, and they are kept apart below:

- **Fixes to the port.** Where the port did something the source does not, correcting it is fidelity. An example is the lamp that lit nothing ([The port](the-port.md)).
- **Interface.** Some things a browser game has to do differently from a 1983 interpreter, such as saving without a file.
- **Presentation.** Most of the pictures, lamps, plates and signs are drawn from what the text already says, and change nothing a typing player would read. Some go beyond the text, and those are listed here, apart from the changes to the game itself.

File references are to `wwwroot/` for the game and to `source/` for Infocom's ZIL. "Round" means a playtest round.

## What the player is told

These are the most numerous. Each adds a line or a description, and leaves the source's own line in place. Where the source answers "nothing special", or prints nothing at all, a new sentence says only what the room text or the object's own words already imply.

- **Descriptions for things that had none.** The magnet, the padlock (and the padlock once acid has fused it: "no key will ever turn in it again"), the conference door, the three goos (each smells of what it tastes of), the funnel-shaped hole, the helicopter, the enunciator panel and the Comm Room's lights. Round four and five decisions of 10 September; the fused padlock is from 12 September. `rules/lower.js:223`, `rules/kalamontee.js:200`, `:255`, `:258`, `rules/survival.js:182`, `rules/tower.js:45`, `:55`, `:131`, `rules/connectors.js:276`.
- **Floyd by name.** Once he has introduced himself, the narrator and the menus call him Floyd, not "multiple purpose robot". A tester took the second name for a second robot (10 Sep, round four; `engine/core.js:64`, `rules/floyd.js:532`).
- **Signs that something worked.** Switching on Floyd adds "you notice a faint hum from somewhere inside the robot" to "Nothing happens." (`rules/floyd.js:252`). An elevator announces its arrival and which side the door is on (`rules/connectors.js:28`). An elevator panel with no card adds that "The slot beside the buttons stays dark" (`:242`). A door or container that changed while you were away is reported when you come back (`engine/core.js:266`). Points scored show as a green notice over the view (`ui/ui.js:203`). All of these are from 10 and 11 September.
- **The clocks explained.** DIAGNOSE says how long each survival clock has left, in hours (`engine/verbs.js:8`, `rules/survival.js:201`). Since 24 September it gives a rough count of moves beside the hours, "about 3 hours (~9 moves)", and so does the Diagnose button. A move is taken as an ordinary step between rooms, 20 minutes, rounded down; the first explanation says that waiting and looking closely use time faster (rounds twenty-one and twenty-two; `engine/core.js:469`). The first hunger warning names DIAGNOSE. Each hunger warning points at food you can reach (`rules/survival.js:131`, `:252`). Tiredness warnings name the dormitories (`:222`, `:403`). A long walk says what it cost (`engine/verbs.js:237`). The status line names the part of the day (`engine/core.js:472`). The shuttle's recording names its hours, and on 18 September a notice under the Kalamontee platform sign gave them too, in the complex's phonetic spelling: "Shutul Servis: evree dae until 6000..." (`rules/connectors.js:350`, `rules/kalamontee.js:455`).
- **What happened in your sleep.** On waking, the game says your things slipped to the floor, and whether the flask or an open canteen spilled. The source empties them without a word (10 Sep; round five for the spills; `rules/survival.js:321-324`). Since 24 September the line names them: "...slipped to the floor beside you: the survival kit, the laser and the teleportation access card." (rounds twenty-one and twenty-two; `rules/survival.js:324`).
- **Why a take failed.** The weight refusal names what to drop (`engine/verbs.js:352`), and the uniform's pocket the first time the limit bites (`:364`). A fumble used to say the things together were too much (round thirteen), which read as weight. Since 24 September it says what the rule is, the number of things in your hands: more than seven, counting what you wear (verbs.zil 527, 552-553). Every time, not only once, it says where things stop counting, the uniform's pocket or an open container you carry, or failing both it names something to drop. The fumble itself is the source's (rounds twenty-one and twenty-two; `engine/verbs.js:334`, `:372`).
- **The hands-full mark.** The Inventory button reads "Inventory (hands full)" once more than seven things are in your hands, the point from which any take may fumble. The source shows the count nowhere (24 Sep, rounds twenty-one and twenty-two; `engine/parser.js:607`).
- **Clues a 1983 player took for granted.** A sound card says it has a magnetic stripe, and a smeared card says so when examined (`rules/lower.js:473`, `:476`). The switched-off Floyd's compartments are mentioned (`rules/floyd.js:258`). The Lawanda map on the Plan Room wall points at the Lower Elevator (`rules/kalamontee.js:531`).
- **Save and restore.** When a player dies with nothing saved, a note after the prompt says that "SAVE costs no time at all, and one would have spared you replaying all of this". The source's own line is untouched (11 Sep, round eleven; `engine/core.js:127`).
- **Other additions.** VERBOSE is on from the start, where the original began in BRIEF (10 Sep; `engine/core.js:55`). The goo says it quenched your thirst too (`rules/survival.js:176`). The crag's exit button on a flooded day says it is the sea (`rules/ship.js:329`).

## Clicks

The click menus are the port's own invention, so most of what they do is interface. Four of them combine two ordinary turns into one click, which a typing player cannot do in one command:

- "Wait for the elevator" (up to twelve ordinary waits, 11 Sep, round seven; `engine/core.js:460`);
- "Open the canteen and drink" (18 Sep, round thirteen; `eatButton`, `engine/parser.js:548`);
- "Take the survival kit and eat", click only, since typing EAT keeps the source's refusal (18 Sep, round fourteen; `eatButton`, `engine/parser.js:557`, `engine/core.js:400`);
- "Drop all except..." (18 Sep, round fourteen; `ui/ui.js:57`);
- "Take the canteen and drink" for the filled canteen left in the kitchen dispenser's niche, and "Take the emergency ration and eat it" for the ration on the floor: the eat button now reaches any food here that one take puts in your hands. The take can still fumble. With that button on screen, the Diagnose button no longer says "nothing to eat" (24 Sep, round twenty-one; `engine/parser.js:582`, `engine/core.js:404`);

and, for a mouse player in the first half of the game, orders to Floyd (11 Sep, round ten; `rules/floyd.js:541`).

**Added on 24 September, from rounds twenty-one and twenty-two.** These change what a click reaches, not what the game does with it. Each is something a typing player could already do:

- **Take out.** A thing inside an open container you carry, such as a spool in the survival kit or the battery in the laser, has a "Take out" entry. Canon's PRE-TAKE allows it, and ITAKE skips the weight check for it (verbs.zil 497-521, 546). The menu used to hide Take on everything you held, at any depth. Fixed parts such as the laser's dial and what sits in the worn uniform's pocket are left out (`engine/parser.js:416`).
- **Put in... reaches into what you carry.** A container's "Put in..." list now offers what stands in an open container you carry, such as the bedistor in the kit for Course Control's cube, as V-PUT would take it. Fixed parts and food are left out, because they would flood the list. Round four had limited it to loose things (`engine/parser.js:494`).
- **The laser takes only a battery.** Its "Put in..." list offers the old or the new battery, carried or lying in the room, as LASER-F takes (comptwo.zil 2673-2688), and nothing when neither is in reach. It used to offer the cards in the uniform pocket. A rule module declares what a container takes (`rules.putAccepts`, `rules/lower.js:392`).
- **Climb only where there is a way.** A stairway offers Climb up or Climb down only for an exit the room has. The Library offered "Climb up" with neither (`engine/parser.js:390`).

**Added on 23 September, from the user's own play as a clicker.** None of these changes what a click does in the game. Each one changes what a click reaches, or where a thing is drawn so that a click can reach it. They are live unless marked. (See also [Playing it as a clicker](playing-as-a-clicker.md).)

- **A click on a name plate opens that thing's menu,** and a plate wins over anything drawn behind it (0f091163). Before this a plate was only a label.
- **A container wins its own clicks.** The filled flask, the medicine bottle and the laser are drawn as pictures, and their contents' click boxes sat in front of them. The contents' boxes now sit just behind the picture, and the contents are reached by taking the container, as the walkthrough does (47de691a).
- **Things in an open container get plates,** such as the fused bedistor in Course Control's cube (47de691a). The cube's plates hang 0.35 m higher, clear of the socket Greg painted (`contentsLabelOffset`, cfe658a4, item 40).
- **Plates that read the painted lettering.** The Lab Office's three buttons are labelled "Lab Liits On", "Lab Liits Of" and "Eemurjensee Sistum" (`plateText`, 47de691a). The words are canon's own, from the room description (comptwo.zil 2236-2238), and the last is the fungicide clue the user could not read at game size. Only the plate changes: the menus and the game's text keep the game's names.
- **Things drawn where they are.** The flask or canteen put under the dispenser stands on the grate under the spout (`placeFor`, a28844d0). Things dropped in Course Control land on open floor, not back inside the cube. The pliers lie on the Tool Room floor, not the bench, because on the bench they were 9 pixels thick. Canon gives them no particular place (2cef0b74).
- **The dispenser's buttons each own their slot** (item 39). Between the Machine Shop's buttons the hover label used to stick on "gray button"; each button now owns its whole slot on the strip, so a sweep across it hovers all nine in turn (f637068a).

**Added on 24 September, from round twenty's blind playtest** (dab910a0). The first two make the click layer agree with itself; the last two offer a click for something the source already answers or allows.

- **Diagnose and the eat button agree about food.** Diagnose read "hungry, nothing to eat" while an "Open the survival kit and eat" button sat beside it: the row counted only food in open containers. One test, `eatButton` (`engine/parser.js:535`), now decides both.
- **Eat on goo in a kit on the floor takes the kit first.** The goo's own menu offered Eat and the source then refused it, "You're not holding the survival kit." The menu's Eat now takes the kit and eats, as two ordinary turns, the same as the quick button (`objectCommand`, `engine/parser.js:409`). Typing EAT keeps the refusal.
- **Take on the celery and Scrub on the slime** on Deck Nine. Infocom wrote answers to both, "The ambassador seems perturbed by your lack of normal protocol." and "Whew. You've cleaned up maybe one ten-thousandth of the slime." (CELERY-F and SLIME-PSEUDO, `globals.zil`), but no menu offered them, so the opening's two jokes could only be typed (`rules/ship.js:312`). "Take all" still leaves the celery alone.
- **"Take with pliers" is offered as soon as you carry the pliers** while the fused bedistor is still in its cube (`rules/lawanda.js:585`). Until then it appeared only after a plain Take had found the bedistor fused, a choice made in round eight and kept in rounds nine and fifteen so that the menu would not solve the puzzle before the player met it. Round twenty's tester nearly gave up there, since the magnet's "Take with magnet" is offered from the start; ***the user chose to offer it at once***.

The same day the playtest harness began refusing a move that names the wrong room ("go down, Balcony" where the stairs lead to the Repair Room), which the parser had been walking on while ignoring the name (`scripts/playtest.mjs:343`). That is testing, not the game.

## What the player sees

The rooms are painted, and a painting has to decide things the text never says. Most of those decisions follow the text closely. The ones below go beyond it. None of them changes anything a typing player would read, but they do change what a clicking player sees.

**Built:**

- **Small things drawn three times their size.** Keys, cards, fuses and the rest are drawn at three times their size in the source. "A deliberate legibility deviation and not a reading of canon": at true size, 25 of 44 things stood under 20 pixels tall where they are usually dropped, and "the user has said three times over that things that small cannot be seen" (DR-117, 21 Sep; `data/characters.json`).
- **Name plates** above the things you can click, which can be moved off what matters (`labelOffset`, 16 Sep).
- **The shuttle's moving tunnel lights**, with the lever, the speed display and a darker grade while moving. These are overlays on the cabin paintings (093214c8, 18 Sep). On 23 September ***the user found it an unexpected and very impressive display***.
- **The long conference table**, once it is painted, and **the pink lamp and button**. Both follow the text edits above.

**Decided on 23 September and built the same day** (the playtest triage in `playtests/2026-09-23/PLAYTEST-LOG.md`, and the user's later notes in `data/playtest-bugs.json`). All of these are live:

- **Lit signs where a card switches something on** (e816cb98). They hang in both elevator cars, the four shuttle cabins, the Mini Booth and Course Control. The lower car's sign can also be seen through its door from the Waiting Area. Greg painted five unlit signs in one style (design-0809), and Norm makes each lit twin by compute (`scripts/sign-lit.mjs`). They are lettered in canon's phonetic spelling: *ELAVAATUR AKTIVAATID*, *SHUTUL KUNTROOLS AKTIVAATID*, *MINITCURIZAASHUN BUUX AKTIVAATID*. This was the user's own proposal: ***a sign that lights up to say the shuttle controls are activated, spelled phonetically like the other signs***. These words are new, though canon has the opposite sign in the teleport booths, "Teleportaashun buux not aktivaatid" (globals.zil 1527). Course Control's sign, *KORS DIIVURJINS MINIMIIZEENG*, is canon's own words (comptwo.zil 522-539). It blinks, and the room's two red fault signs go out when the course is fixed. They replace green placeholder boxes, which had taught the user something false: ***they believed they had used the upper elevator card in the first elevator and left it there***. ## Not the game: interface and testing

- **Checkpoints refresh the player.** A checkpoint (born 23 September; see the [journal](journal.md)) is a real route of commands replayed to a position. On load, the player is set fed, rested and well, at the user's word: ***every checkpoint should start the player fully healthy and rested***. The code says so: "Not in the source" (`rules/survival.js:352-364`). This happens only on a checkpoint load. No one playing from the start ever meets it.
- **Save and restore** keep one slot inside the game, where the original asked for a file. RESTART and QUIT point at the page's restart link instead of asking yes or no (`engine/verbs.js:113`, `:125`). Since 24 September a won game offers Restore too, as canon's FINISH does after a win as after a death (verbs.zil 252-279). The port had hidden it (round twenty-two; `engine/core.js:413`, `ui/ui.js:200`).
- **The blind testers' option numbers.** In the text harness, a list of three or more targets for one tool collapses into a submenu. Since 24 September a submenu, once shown in a room, stays a submenu there, so its number keeps its meaning when the targets fall below three (round twenty-one; `scripts/playtest.mjs:164`).
- **"Play from here"** (`?room=ID`, 22 Sep) drops a reviewer into a room, for checking continuity. It is not part of a game.
- **Checkpoints for testing one fix.** By the evening of 23 September there were 17 checkpoints, among them one per bug the user wanted to retest (`TC17-course-fix` and the like, a2fe780a). The dashboard's playtest checklist links each fix to the checkpoint where its test begins. They are testing aids, like the rest of this section.
- **Only when run locally.** Since 24 September the published site has none of these: no debug link or panel, no checkpoints, and `?room=` and `?checkpoint=` do nothing there. At the user's word, ***the debug and dashboard links may show when the game is run locally, but never on the published site*** (`scripts/build-site.mjs`, `game.js:25`).
- **Real clicks in the tests.** Since 23 September the browser harness opens every menu by clicking where the thing is drawn. A target must be at least 12 by 12 pixels on screen, and three quarters of a 9 by 9 grid of clicks over it must reach it (0f091163). This measures the page. It does not change the game.
